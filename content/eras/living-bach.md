After [the Gesellschaft](../events/revival/bach-gesellschaft-1850.md), Bach's survival was assured; the remaining history — this arc — is the story of **what each era needed him to be**, and the timeline's ribbon here becomes a gallery of portraits, each true, none complete.

**The Romantic Bach** was a cathedral: massed choirs for the Passions, [Liszt and Busoni's](../events/living-bach/romantic-transcriptions.md) thunderous piano transcriptions, the Air renamed for [a violin's G string](../works/leipzig/bwv-1068.md), Gounod's Ave Maria floating over [the first prelude](../works/cothen/bwv-846.md) — grandeur, sentiment, and the organist as mystic. Against it, around 1900, came the first correctives: **Schweitzer** reading [the cantatas' pictorial language](../events/living-bach/schweitzer-1905.md) and demanding clearer, plainer performance; **[Casals](../people/pablo-casals.md)** [pulling the cello suites out of the étude drawer](../events/living-bach/casals-suites-1890.md); Landowska returning the harpsichord to the argument (her deathless quip to a pianist: *"You play Bach your way, and I'll play him his way"*).

The twentieth century then split him productively in two. **The modernists' Bach** was pure structure: [Webern orchestrating the six-voice ricercar](../works/leipzig/musical-offering.md) as proto-serialism, Stravinsky arranging [the Canonic Variations](../works/leipzig/bwv-769.md), and in 1955 [a young Canadian's Goldberg recording](../events/living-bach/gould-1955.md) making contrapuntal clarity itself a mass-market thrill. **The historians' Bach** — the *historically informed performance* movement — asked instead what the music sounded like in its own rooms: [Harnoncourt and Leonhardt's period-instrument complete cantatas](../events/living-bach/hip-1971.md) (1971–89) rebuilt the sound world; Rifkin's one-voice-per-part thesis pushed the question to its edge; and by century's end the movement's second generation had made "period Bach" simply *normal* Bach.

Which brings the arc to [your requested modern voices](../events/living-bach/gardiner-pilgrimage-2000.md). **John Eliot [Gardiner](../people/john-eliot-gardiner.md)'s** Bach Cantata Pilgrimage (2000) performed all the surviving sacred cantatas on their own liturgical days, in churches across Europe, in one year — the [cantata machine](../events/leipzig-1/cantata-machine.md) re-experienced as a lived calendar — and his *Music in the Castle of Heaven* (2013) gave this timeline much of its human Bach. **Masaaki Suzuki's** Bach Collegium Japan spent eighteen years recording every sacred cantata — Lutheran Leipzig's weekly bread, perfected in Kobe: the arc's best proof that this music's citizenship is now planetary. **Koopman**, **Herreweghe**, the Netherlands Bach Society's free-to-all [*All of Bach*](../BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) film archive — completeness, once a scholars' dream, is now a listener's starting point.

And one object extends the arc past the planet: in 1977, [the Voyager Golden Record](../events/living-bach/voyager-1977.md) left Earth carrying three Bach works — more than any other composer — including [the Brandenburg No. 2](../works/cothen/bwv-1047.md) as track one. The biologist Lewis Thomas, asked what humanity should send, gave the arc its epigraph: *"I would vote for Bach, all of Bach... we would be bragging, but it is surely excusable."* The ethereal streams of this timeline's design — music flowing into the forever — are, since 1977, not a metaphor.

**Study anchor:** the [Pilgrimage card](../events/living-bach/gardiner-pilgrimage-2000.md) and the [HIP card](../events/living-bach/hip-1971.md) — between them, the performance choices behind every recording this timeline recommends.
