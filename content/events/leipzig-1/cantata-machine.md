Bach's first cantata as cantor — *Die Elenden sollen essen*, BWV 75, a fourteen-movement double-length statement of intent — sounded on 30 May 1723. Then the calendar took over, and for roughly two years it never let go.

**The weekly cycle**, reconstructed from the surviving performing materials: choose or receive the text (tied to each Sunday's prescribed Gospel); compose — realistically Monday to Wednesday; the household copying shop ([Anna Magdalena](../../people/anna-magdalena-bach.md), the elder sons, senior students) extracts the performing parts; one rehearsal, likely Saturday; performance Sunday morning in the Thomaskirche or Nikolaikirche in strict alternation, within a liturgy running from seven o'clock toward noon — the cantata as the sermon's musical twin, on that day's Gospel. Feast days added more; Christmas week could demand several works in days. Dürr's chronology — built from paper types, copyists' hands, and ink — shows what the Obituary only asserts: cycle after cycle, week after week, **there was no pause**.

The **first cycle** (1723–24) mixed new works with revived Weimar cantatas — the veteran housebuilder reusing sound timber. The **second** (Trinity 1724 to spring 1725) is the summit: the *chorale cantata* cycle, every work built on a single hymn — its melody anchoring the opening chorus (usually a vast chorale fantasia), its inner verses paraphrased into arias and recitatives, its final verse closing plainly — some forty of them, each structurally individual. **This is the timeline's single most concentrated exhibit of what Bach was.**

**Why it matters.** Every cantata you study was made *inside this machine* — which is why craft (formulas, models, parody, the chorale's scaffolding) and inspiration cannot be separated in Bach: the deadline forbade the distinction. [Gardiner](../../people/john-eliot-gardiner.md), who performed the cycle on its own liturgical calendar across a year ([the 2000 Pilgrimage](../../eras/living-bach.md)), calls the second cycle's consistency simply beyond explanation. The R3 catalog will walk it cantata by cantata.
