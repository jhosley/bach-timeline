Bach's eyesight — strained by a lifetime of small notes by candlelight — failed badly through 1749; the council, with exquisite tact, held a **pre-audition for his successor** that June, while he lived and worked. In late March and early April 1750 he submitted to two eye operations by **John "Chevalier" Taylor**, a traveling English oculist of maximal self-promotion and dubious technique, then passing through Leipzig (his later clientele included Handel, with the same result). The surgeries and their brutal aftercare left Bach fully blind and gravely weakened. The Obituary supplies the last scenes — ten days before the end, his sight briefly, inexplicably returned; hours later, a stroke — and the deathbed tradition (dictating a final chorale, *Vor deinen Thron tret ich hiermit*, "Before thy throne I now appear"; flagged *tradition*, the truth gentler and messier: he revised an existing setting, probably earlier). He died on the evening of **28 July 1750**, sixty-five years old, and was buried three days later at St. John's cemetery — an oak coffin, recorded; **no gravestone**.

The aftermath moved fast on every track. The council filled the post within weeks (a burgomaster, immortally: the school needed *a cantor, not a Kapellmeister*). The estate — instruments, the library, a modest sum — was divided among nine surviving heirs; the **manuscripts scattered** with the sons ([Arc II opens with the consequences](../../eras/eclipse.md)); Anna Magdalena, with the youngest children, slid into [her alms-supported decade](../../people/anna-magdalena-bach.md). The grave's location was lost within a generation — rediscovered by excavation only in **1894**, the remains moved in 1950 to the Thomaskirche choir, where they lie under a bronze slab that is now among the most visited spots in Leipzig.

**Why it matters.** Arc I ends with every theme in view: the working conditions (blind from the job's small print, replaced with relief), the family economy breaking, the manuscripts beginning their century of dispersal — and, in that unmarked grave against the later bronze slab, the entire reception history this timeline exists to tell, compressed into two burials.
