In June 1747 Bach finally joined the **Correspondirende Societät der musicalischen Wissenschaften** — the learned society founded by his former student Lorenz Mizler to treat music as a science, its members (Telemann and Handel among them) exchanging scholarly papers by post. Membership required two submissions, and Bach's choices are a self-portrait in two media.

The musical offering: the ***Canonic Variations on "Vom Himmel hoch"*** (BWV 769) — the Christmas hymn run through five movements of escalating strict canon for organ, ending with a movement stacking the chorale against itself in augmentation and diminution. Erudition, on the society's terms, but *singing* — the Christmas tune audible throughout the mathematics.

The visual: the **Haussmann portrait** (1746) — the only certainly authentic image of Bach — in which the cantor, stern in civic dress, holds a sheet of music toward the viewer: a **triplex canon for six voices** (BWV 1076), notated in riddle form. Not a keyboard, not a church, not a family: a *puzzle in counterpoint*, extended like a calling card. Asked by history what he was, Bach answered in his own iconography: a learned musician ([Wolff's title](../../BIBLIOGRAPHY.md), taken from exactly here).

A famous embellishment, flagged *tradition*: that Bach delayed joining until he could be **member number fourteen** — B+A+C+H in number-alphabet = 14, the cipher some analysts chase through his works. He *was* the fourteenth member; the intent is undocumented, and number-symbol hunting in Bach ranges from suggestive to numerological fantasy (the R4 craft essays will draw the sober line).

**Why it matters.** Late Bach's self-definition, documented: while fashion filed him under the past ([Scheibe](../leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md)), he filed himself under *science* — canon as demonstrable truth. The portrait is effectively the timeline's frontispiece; the app should hang it exactly here.
