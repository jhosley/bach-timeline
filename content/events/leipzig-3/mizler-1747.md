In June 1747, a month after the Potsdam adventure, Bach finally joined the Correspondirende Societät der musicalischen Wissenschaften — the Corresponding Society of Musical Sciences, founded by his former student Lorenz Mizler on a thoroughly Enlightenment premise: that music was a science, and could be treated like one. The society had no concert hall and no choir; its members — [Telemann](../../people/telemann.md) among them, and Handel — exchanged scholarly papers by post, a learned republic of letters conducted in counterpoint and treatise. That a working cantor buried in civic obligations should want a seat in it at all tells us something. What he chose to send tells us more, because membership required submissions, and Bach's amount to a self-portrait in two media.

The musical credential was the *Canonic Variations on "Vom Himmel hoch"* (BWV 769): the familiar Christmas hymn run through five movements of escalating strict canon for organ, culminating in a movement that stacks the chorale against itself in augmentation and diminution — the same melody moving at different speeds simultaneously, and agreeing with itself at every checkpoint. It is erudition precisely on the society's terms, music as demonstrable proposition. But the crucial fact about the piece is that it *sings*: the Christmas tune stays audible all the way through the mathematics. Bach's idea of science never stopped being music you could follow with your ear.

The visual credential has become the most familiar object in all of Bach iconography, though its origin here is easily forgotten. The Haussmann portrait of 1746 — the only certainly authentic image of Bach we possess — shows the cantor stern in civic dress, holding a sheet of music out toward the viewer. The sheet is not a cantata, not a chorale, not anything connected to church, keyboard, or family. It is a triplex canon for six voices (BWV 1076), notated in riddle form: a puzzle in counterpoint, extended toward posterity like a calling card. Asked by history what he was, Bach answered in his own chosen iconography — a learned musician. Christoph Wolff's standard biography [takes its very title](../../BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) from exactly this self-description; every reader of the modern scholarship is looking, in effect, at that outstretched sheet.

One famous embellishment needs its flag. The story runs that Bach deliberately delayed joining the society until he could be member number fourteen — because in the number-alphabet, where A is one and B is two, B+A+C+H sums to 14, the cipher some analysts pursue through his works. The record shows he *was* the fourteenth member; the intention is entirely undocumented, and the timeline marks the story *tradition*. It is worth adding the wider caution here, because this card is where the numbers enter: number-symbol hunting in Bach ranges from the genuinely suggestive to numerological fantasy, and the craft essays will try to draw the sober line between them. The fourteen is a real and pleasing coincidence. Whether it was ever anything more, no document says.

What makes this quiet, letter-writing episode matter is the timing. These are the years in which fashionable opinion — [Scheibe's attack](../leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md) had said it in print a decade earlier — filed Bach under the past: turgid, over-worked, out of step with the natural new style. Bach's response, here made documentary, was to file himself under *science*. Not old-fashioned but permanent; not against fashion but beneath it, where canon is demonstrable truth and a proposition in counterpoint holds regardless of the decade's taste. The Canonic Variations and the riddle canon in the painted hand are the same statement in two media, made the same year the Musical Offering answered a king: this is what I am, and it does not expire.

The portrait, for that reason, is effectively the timeline's frontispiece, and it belongs exactly here — not at the birth, not at the death, but at the moment its subject chose the pose. Most composers are portrayed with their instrument. Bach had himself painted with an argument.
