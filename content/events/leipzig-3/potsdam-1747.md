On the evening of 7 May 1747, as [Frederick the Great](../../people/frederick-the-great.md) readied his nightly chamber concert at Potsdam (the philosopher-warrior-king played serious flute, [C.P.E. Bach](../weimar/weimar-family-births.md) accompanying), the list of arrivals was brought in. "Gentlemen," the king announced — the anecdote is Wilhelm Friedemann's via Forkel, its core corroborated by the Berlin newspapers — **"old Bach is come."** The concert was cancelled. The sixty-two-year-old cantor, still in traveling clothes, was walked through the palace rooms to try the king's collection of **Silbermann fortepianos** — the newfangled hammer-action instruments Frederick collected by the dozen — improvising as he went.

Then the test: the king played him a theme — long, chromatic, sinking through a diminished world; whether Frederick's own or planted (Emanuel's fingerprints are suspected; *disputed*) — and asked for a fugue on it, extempore. Bach delivered one in three voices, to astonishment. Asked next for a **six-voice fugue** on the royal subject, he declined — not every theme bears six voices, he said — and improvised a six-part fugue on a theme of his own instead. Next day, per the papers: an organ recital at Potsdam's Heiliggeistkirche.

Home in Leipzig, unsatisfied by honor alone, he spent two months building the answer he *couldn't* improvise: the ***[Musical Offering](../../works/leipzig/musical-offering.md)*** — the royal theme worked into two ricercars (three-voice and, yes, **six-voice**, the summit of his keyboard counterpoint), ten canons (several as riddles for the court to solve), and a trio sonata in the king's own galant taste with the theme smuggled through it — engraved and dedicated to Frederick in July 1747. No reply from the king is recorded.

**Why it matters.** The Enlightenment's future meeting counterpoint's past, in one candle-lit room — the timeline's richest single scene, and Arc III returns to its six-voice ricercar [aboard Webern's orchestration and the modern canon](../../eras/living-bach.md). The R3 card unpacks the Offering piece by piece.
