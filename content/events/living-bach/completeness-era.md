The arc's quiet final revolution has no single evening attached: sometime in the last generation, **completeness became normal**. **Masaaki Suzuki** and his Kobe-founded **Bach Collegium Japan** recorded every sacred cantata for BIS across eighteen years (1995–2013, with the secular works following) — polished, luminous, and profoundly moving as a *fact*: Leipzig's weekly Lutheran bread, perfected in Japan by a conductor for whom this music's theology is personal, the definitive proof that Bach's citizenship is now planetary. **Ton Koopman's** Amsterdam cycle ran in parallel; **Herreweghe** deepened the choral core; and every one of them stands on [the 1971 revolution's](hip-1971.md) shoulders.

Then the internet completed [the Gesellschaft's](../revival/bach-gesellschaft-1850.md) logic: the **Netherlands Bach Society** — the world's oldest Bach ensemble — began *All of Bach* in 2013: **every work, filmed in performance, released free online** ([the bibliography's standing recommendation](../../BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) for *watching* this music). Where a Leipzig burgher heard a cantata once and never again, and [Mozart had to spread the parts on his knees](../eclipse/mozart-motet-1789.md), any student anywhere now summons three rival performances of anything before breakfast. Custody's three-century story — [scattered](../eclipse/estate-dispersal-1750.md), [collected](../revival/bach-gesellschaft-1850.md), [sounded](hip-1971.md) — ends in universal access.

**Why it matters.** This is the era *you* study in, and its abundance is a new kind of problem — which is, frankly, why this timeline exists: when everything is available, the scarce resources are **context and sequence**. The eras, cards, and craft essays here are one answer; the completeness era supplies the recordings; your practice supplies the rest.
