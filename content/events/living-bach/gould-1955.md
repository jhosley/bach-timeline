Columbia's producers thought the repertoire choice was commercial suicide: an unknown twenty-two-year-old Canadian insisting his **debut LP** be [the Goldberg Variations](../../works/leipzig/goldberg-variations.md) — in 1955 a connoisseur's curiosity, "harpsichord music," associated with [Landowska's](../../eras/living-bach.md) grand priestess manner. **Glenn Gould** arrived at the East 30th Street studio in June with his sawed-down chair and his humming, and recorded the performance that changed Bach's twentieth-century address: fast, feather-light, staccato-etched — **every contrapuntal line audible as a separate living thing** — the [canons](../../craft/craft-04-canon.md) not solemn but *exhilarating*. The record became a genuine bestseller, and a generation discovered that counterpoint could hit like jazz.

Gould's whole career then ran on Bach ([the WTC](../../works/leipzig/wtc-2.md), [the partitas](../../works/leipzig/partitas.md), [his beloved II/22](../../works/leipzig/bwv-891.md)) and on a modernist creed: concert halls were obsolete; the recording *was* the artwork — splices, close mics, and all. He quit the stage at thirty-one and made the studio his instrument, a decision that looks more prophetic every streaming decade. Then the frame closed with uncanny symmetry: in **1981** he re-recorded the Goldbergs — slower, inward, x-rayed — and died months after its release, at fifty. The two recordings, twenty-six years apart, are the modern era's own [two-books-of-the-WTC](../../works/leipzig/wtc-2.md): one artist, one work, youth and late style, permanently comparable.

**Why it matters.** The modernists' Bach at maximum reach — structure as ecstasy, no cathedral required — and the single recording most likely to have brought any living listener (possibly you) to this timeline's composer. Track one of the case that Bach is *contemporary* music.
