The Romantic century loved Bach the way it loved cathedrals — and renovated him accordingly. Its instrument of devotion was the **transcription**: **Liszt** carried [the great organ preludes and fugues](../../works/weimar/bwv-543.md) onto the concert piano; **Busoni's** version of [the Chaconne](../../works/cothen/bwv-1004.md) turned one violin's implied polyphony into full-orchestral pianism (still a repertoire staple, and still an argument: is it revelation or replacement?); the violinist **Wilhelmj** dropped [the Air](../../works/leipzig/bwv-1068.md) onto the violin's lowest string and accidentally renamed it; **Gounod** floated an Ave Maria over [the C-major prelude](../../works/cothen/bwv-846.md); Stokowski's orchestral [Toccata and Fugue](../../works/arnstadt/bwv-565.md) waited at the century's far end. Meanwhile the Passions swelled to festival choirs of hundreds, and organists cultivated the "Bach style" as sacred thunder.

It is easy — the twentieth century found it *very* easy — to catalogue the distortions: scale inflated, textures thickened, [dance-feet](../../craft/craft-05-chorale.md) buried under velvet. The fairer reading, which this timeline takes: **transcription was how a century without recordings circulated music it loved** — every arrangement a delivery vehicle, and the distortions were the postage. Brahms studying [the Gesellschaft volumes](../revival/bach-gesellschaft-1850.md) and writing a left-hand Chaconne study "to feel what Bach felt"; Liszt bringing organ counterpoint to audiences a thousand miles from a great organ — this is reception as metabolism, not vandalism.

**Why it matters.** First portrait in Arc III's gallery, and the baseline the twentieth century's [historians](hip-1971.md) and [modernists](gould-1955.md) each rebelled against — in opposite directions. When you compare a 1900s-style Matthew Passion with [Gardiner's](gardiner-pilgrimage-2000.md), you are hearing this card argued with.
