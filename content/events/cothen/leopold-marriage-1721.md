On 11 December 1721 — eight days after Bach's own wedding — Prince Leopold married his cousin, Princess Friederica Henrietta of Anhalt-Bernburg. Writing to his old friend Georg Erdmann nine years later, Bach diagnosed the turn his life took with a single borrowed Latin word: he had meant to stay at Cöthen for life, "but it happened that the said Serenissimus married a Bernburg princess, and then it appeared as though the musical inclination of the said Prince had become somewhat lukewarm, especially since the new Princess seemed to be an **amusa**" — a stranger to the muses.

Whether the princess deserves the blame is a fair question (the timeline notes it as Bach's own, possibly ungenerous, attribution): Anhalt-Cöthen also faced real fiscal pressure in these years, including Prussian demands, and the Capelle's budget was an obvious place to cut. Either way the trend was unmistakable to the man watching his ensemble shrink — and within a year Bach was pursuing the Leipzig cantorate. The princess died in April 1723, just as Bach was securing Leipzig; by then the decision was made.

**Why it matters.** This is the clearest documented case of Bach explaining **his own career reasoning** — the Erdmann letter is the closest thing we have to Bach narrating his life, and this timeline quotes it at every point it touches. It also completes the Cöthen lesson: an artist's golden age can depend on something as fragile as one patron's attention.
