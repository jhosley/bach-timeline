The Obituary records two orbits from the Lüneburg base, both formative, neither documented beyond the family's telling — hence the *tradition* flag on the details, though neither claim is doubted in substance.

**North to Hamburg** (thirty miles, "several times," on foot): to St. Catherine's, where **Johann Adam Reincken** presided over one of the legendary organs of Europe — four manuals, a 32-foot principal in the facade (a pipe speaking two octaves below the bass staff; more architecture than instrument). Reincken's own playing embodied the *stylus phantasticus* — the free, theatrical, improvisatory manner of the northern school. Bach later testified to the organ's glory in detail (he praised its reeds all his life), and [the 1720 return visit](../cothen/hamburg-reincken-1720.md) completes the story.

**South to Celle** (fifty miles): Duke Georg Wilhelm kept a court modeled on Versailles, orchestra included — Lully's bowing, French dance idioms, the *agréments* (the codified French ornament vocabulary). The Obituary says Bach heard the band often enough to absorb the style thoroughly; scholars suspect the school's noble connections (or service as an accompanist) supplied the access.

**Why it matters.** Locate on the education track what each pole supplied: the north gave him the **organ** — scale, fantasy, the sacred sublime; Celle gave him **France** — dance, ornament, orchestral discipline. Add Ohrdruf's Pachelbel line and, still to come, [Italy by transcription in Weimar](../weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md), and you have the complete recipe: Bach never left Germany, but by twenty he had assembled all of Europe on foot and by hand. The synthesis *was* the genius.
