Music history's most crowded nursery: **George Frideric Handel**, born 23 February 1685 in Halle — about eighty miles from Eisenach; **Domenico Scarlatti**, born 26 October 1685 in Naples; and Bach between them in March.

The three lives make a controlled experiment in what a musician born in 1685 could become. Handel left Germany at eighteen — Italy, then London — and became an international opera entrepreneur, celebrity, and eventually an English national institution, dead a wealthy man in Westminster Abbey. Scarlatti followed the courts to Portugal and Spain and poured his maturity into 555 harpsichord sonatas for a queen. Bach never left a 200-mile patch of central Germany, never wrote an opera, never met either of them — though he twice tried to meet Handel on his visits home to Halle, missing him both times (the second attempt, in 1729, is on the Leipzig track). One-way admiration, apparently: Bach owned and performed Handel's music; no evidence Handel returned the attention.

**Why it matters.** Contemporaries would have ranked them Handel, then Scarlatti, then the provincial organist — the exact inverse of the posthumous verdict Arc II traces. Keep the class of 1685 in mind whenever this timeline shows Bach *choosing* the narrower path: the wider one was demonstrably available to a Saxon of his generation and gifts, and he watched a hometown near-neighbor take it all the way to Westminster.
