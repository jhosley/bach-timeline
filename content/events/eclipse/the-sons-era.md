When the English music historian Charles Burney toured Germany in 1772 gathering material for his great survey, the Bach he traveled to meet was **Carl Philipp Emanuel** — by then in [Telemann's old Hamburg post](../../people/telemann.md) (godfather's chair passing to godson), author of the era's definitive keyboard treatise, and, by the public consensus of the age, *the great Bach*: the master of the sensitive style, the bridge every history draws between the Baroque and Haydn. **Johann Christian**, meanwhile, owned London — opera, royal music-mastership, the concert series that defined English musical life — and when an eight-year-old prodigy named Mozart visited in 1764, it was J.C. Bach who sat him on his knee and shaped his sense of what a singing allegro could be. Sebastian, in this world, was a *father figure* in the most literal sense: the stern old contrapuntist from whom the moderns descended, respected the way one respects a grandfather's portrait.

The sons' own attitudes were loyal and complicated at once. Emanuel curated the legacy faithfully — [co-writing the Obituary](../../events/leipzig-3/death-1750.md), preserving manuscripts, answering [Forkel's](forkel-1802.md) inquiries — while his own aesthetic ("play from the soul, not like a trained bird") half-agreed with [the Scheibe verdict](../../events/leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md) on strict counterpoint. Friedemann, the most gifted and the most like his father as an organist, traded ever more heavily on the name as his fortunes sank. None of the four composing sons wrote in the father's idiom; all of them carried his training visibly. The clan's six-generation machine produced one last, brilliant, divergent generation — and then, with their deaths (Friedemann 1784, Emanuel 1788, Christian 1782), stopped.

**Why it matters.** The eclipse's engine, seen precisely: Sebastian wasn't rejected — he was *superseded within his own family*, which history's fashion-cycle treated as natural succession. The revival, when it comes, must reverse not hostility but **filial success** — and its first instruments (Emanuel's preserved manuscripts, the sons' testimony to Forkel) were built by the very generation that overshadowed him.
