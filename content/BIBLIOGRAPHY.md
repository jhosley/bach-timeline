# Bibliography & Sources

The master source list for the Bach Timeline. Every short citation in a content file's `sources` field (e.g. `Wolff, The Learned Musician, ch. 7`) resolves to a full entry here. The app will render this as a **Sources & Further Reading** section, and each card's footnotes will link into it.

Links policy: book links point to a purchase page (Amazon) verified to resolve at the date noted; public-domain sources point to free full texts; online resources point to their canonical URLs. All links re-verified during phase R6.

## Primary sources (documents from Bach's lifetime and circle)

### The New Bach Reader
Hans T. David and Arthur Mendel, eds.; revised and enlarged by Christoph Wolff. *The New Bach Reader: A Life of Johann Sebastian Bach in Letters and Documents.* New York: W. W. Norton, 1998.
**What it is:** the surviving documents in English — council minutes, contracts, Bach's letters (including the 1730 Erdmann letter), the Obituary of 1754, early reception documents. When a card cites a council record or letter, this is where to read it whole.
**Link:** _pending verification_

### Forkel's biography (1802)
Johann Nikolaus Forkel. *Johann Sebastian Bach: His Life, Art, and Work* (orig. *Ueber Johann Sebastian Bachs Leben, Kunst und Kunstwerke*, 1802). English trans. Charles Sanford Terry, 1920.
**What it is:** the first Bach biography, built on correspondence with Bach's sons — invaluable and openly partisan (Forkel's nationalist motives are themselves part of the Arc II story). Public domain.
**Link:** _pending verification_

## The modern scholarly spine

### Wolff — the standard biography
Christoph Wolff. *Johann Sebastian Bach: The Learned Musician.* New York: W. W. Norton, 2000; updated ed. 2013.
**What it is:** the standard scholarly life — meticulous on documents, employment, and institutions. This timeline's default authority for biographical fact; chapter citations throughout refer to this book.
**Link:** _pending verification_

### Gardiner — the modern portrait
John Eliot Gardiner. *Bach: Music in the Castle of Heaven: A Portrait of Johann Sebastian Bach.* New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 2013.
**What it is:** the conductor's Bach — vivid, opinionated, humanizing (the unruly schoolboy, the fractious employee, the visceral theologian), strongest on the vocal works from a lifetime of performing them. This timeline's voice for interpretation is calibrated against it; cited wherever a reading (rather than a fact) is in play.
**Link:** _pending verification_

### Williams — the skeptical counterweight
Peter Williams. *J. S. Bach: A Life in Music.* Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007.
**What it is:** a deliberately source-skeptical biography that questions comfortable traditions — the conscience behind this timeline's *Documented / Tradition / Disputed* flags.
**Link:** _pending verification_

### Dürr — the cantata bible
Alfred Dürr. *The Cantatas of J. S. Bach.* Trans. Richard D. P. Jones. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2005.
**What it is:** the standard work-by-work reference for the cantatas — dating, occasion, text, analysis. The R3 cantata cards' primary authority.
**Link:** _pending verification_

Laurence Dreyfus. *Bach and the Patterns of Invention.* Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1996.
