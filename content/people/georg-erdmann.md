The friend of Bach's youth — companion on the 1700 walk from Ohrdruf to Lüneburg and fellow Mettenchor scholar — who left music for law and diplomacy, ending as Imperial Russian resident (consul) in Danzig.

His importance to posterity is outsized: because Erdmann was far away and trusted, Bach wrote him **letters** — and Bach's surviving personal letters are vanishingly rare. The great one, of 28 October 1730, is the single most quoted document in Bach biography: in it Bach reviews his whole career ("From my youth up... I was destined for music"), explains the move from Cöthen ([the *amusa* passage](../events/cothen/leopold-marriage-1721.md)), confesses his status anxieties about the cantorate, complains precisely about Leipzig's costs, his employers ("strange authorities with little devotion to music"), and — half-seriously — asks Erdmann to find him a job in Danzig. The composer of the [St. Matthew Passion](../works/leipzig/st-matthew-passion.md), at the height of his powers, was quietly asking a school friend to help him leave.

A person card for a man with no music in the story — because without this friendship, the timeline would lose its best first-person source for how Bach understood his own life.
