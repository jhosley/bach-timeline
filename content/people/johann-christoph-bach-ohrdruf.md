Not to be confused with the "profound" Eisenach cousin of the same name: this Johann Christoph was Sebastian's eldest brother — organist of St. Michael's, Ohrdruf, for thirty years, and the man who raised him.

He studied three years with Pachelbel in Erfurt, took the Ohrdruf post at nineteen, married in 1694 — and within a year of the wedding inherited two orphaned brothers. He fed, schooled, and trained Sebastian from ten to fifteen; whatever the moonlight-manuscript story says about his strictness, the outcome speaks for the teaching, and the relationship stayed warm: Sebastian later trained Johann Christoph's own sons in return, and the brothers' families remained interwoven for decades.

His personal manuscript collections (the *Andreas Bach Book* and *Möller Manuscript*, compiled in Ohrdruf) survive, and they are precious: they contain some of the earliest copies of Sebastian's own compositions, our best evidence for what the young Bach was writing before any court or consistory took notice. The guardian-brother, fittingly, became the first archivist of the composer he made possible.
