Founder of the Monteverdi Choir (1964) and the English Baroque Soloists (1978), and for half a century one of the defining Bach interpreters alive: rhythmically fierce, dance-rooted, dramatically vivid — [the HIP revolution's](../events/living-bach/hip-1971.md) second generation at full confidence. His grandparents' farmhouse, by family legend he loves to tell, sheltered the great Haussmann portrait of Bach ([the canon-holding one](../events/leipzig-3/mizler-1747.md)) during the Second World War — the future conductor grew up, literally, under Bach's gaze. (*Tradition* flag on the aura; the painting's wartime residence is documented.)

[The 2000 Cantata Pilgrimage](../events/living-bach/gardiner-pilgrimage-2000.md) is his monument — all the sacred cantatas, on their own Sundays, in one year, self-released on his SDG label when the industry balked — and ***Music in the Castle of Heaven*** (2013) its written companion: the performing musician's biography, impatient with plaster saints, alert to [the unruly documented Bach](../events/arnstadt/geyersbach-1705.md), and eloquent on what it *feels like* to conduct this music at its own pace. This timeline cites him wherever interpretation (rather than document) is at stake, and its default cantata recommendations are his — a deliberate design choice: the study companion's catalog and its living-tradition story should reinforce each other, and no one embodies that union more completely.
