Sebastian's father was a working musician of the sturdiest clan type: director of the Eisenach town music (Stadtpfeifer) and court trumpeter to the duke — a double appointment covering everything from tower fanfares and wedding bands to court service. A surviving portrait (the only image of anyone in Bach's childhood household) shows a robust, dark-eyed man; contemporaries record his skill on violin and trumpet.

He was also a twin — so identical to his brother Johann Christoph of Arnstadt that (family legend, via the genealogy) their own wives told them apart with difficulty. The twins' bond is the clan's tenderest story; Ambrosius's death months after his brother's fits the family's own telling of it.

His significance for the timeline: Sebastian's **first teacher**, almost certainly on violin and the fundamentals of the trade. The boy who later wrote the most demanding string music of the century ([Sei Solo](../works/cothen/sei-solo.md)) grew up in a professional string-player's workshop. His death in February 1695, nine months after his wife's, orphaned Sebastian at nine and set the whole biography in motion.
