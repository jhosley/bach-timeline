Europe's model modern monarch — philosopher-correspondent of Voltaire, military gambler, flute virtuoso — who kept one of the continent's finest musical establishments at Potsdam with C.P.E. Bach at the keyboard and nightly chamber concerts led from the royal flute. His taste was firmly *galant*: melody, elegance, feeling — the very fashion that filed old Bach's counterpoint under the past ([Scheibe's aesthetics](../events/leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md), crowned).

His one evening with the elder Bach ([May 1747](../events/leipzig-3/potsdam-1747.md)) became the most famous scene of the composer's life: the royal theme, the improvised fugues on the new Silbermann fortepianos, and the *[Musical Offering](../works/leipzig/musical-offering.md)* that followed — a monument of the art Frederick's own era was discarding, dedicated to him, and apparently never acknowledged. Decades later, to a visitor, the old king could still sing the theme from memory and tell the story of "old Bach" — the anecdote reaching us through the diplomat's diary: patron and monument had quietly exchanged places.

For the timeline he is the perfect closing foil of Arc I: absolute power, modern taste, and historical glamour — outlived, in the only ledger this app keeps, by a cantor's answer to a party trick.
