Toronto-born, self-exiled from the concert stage at thirty-one, and the twentieth century's most influential Bach pianist — possibly its most influential Bach *listener*: Gould heard the counterpoint as living argument and made records on which, for the first time for many listeners, **every voice in a fugue is separately, thrillingly audible**. [The 1955 Goldberg debut](../events/living-bach/gould-1955.md) made him famous; the studio-hermit decades (humming, splicing, broadcasting, writing) made him a prophet of exactly the recorded, on-demand musical world [the completeness era](../events/living-bach/completeness-era.md) delivered; [the 1981 Goldberg remake](../events/living-bach/gould-1955.md) — released weeks before his death at fifty — closed the frame with late-style inwardness.

His Bach was unapologetically *his*: piano not harpsichord, tempos from another physics, repeats dropped, romance banished — the [historically informed movement's](../events/living-bach/hip-1971.md) exact contemporary and exact opposite, and the standing proof that the two roads both reach the summit. His [WTC II C-major](../works/leipzig/bwv-870.md) rides [the Voyager record](../events/living-bach/voyager-1977.md); his [beloved II/22](../works/leipzig/bwv-891.md) anchors that card. For a study companion, he is the essential *comparison term*: hear any work here in Gould **and** in a period recording, and the gap between them is a free education in everything interpretation decides.
