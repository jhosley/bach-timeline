A fugue is not a form. That's the first correction this essay owes you, because the textbook diagram — exposition, episode, entry, stretto, coda — suggests a mold into which music is poured, like sonata form. Better to say: **a fugue is a procedure, a discipline, a way for several voices to discuss one subject** — and what happens after the opening is as free as an essay after its thesis paragraph. Bach's fugues share machinery, not floor plans; the [Well-Tempered Clavier's](../works/cothen/wtc-1.md) forty-eight are precisely a demonstration that the procedure yields as many shapes as there are subjects.

The machinery, defined once, plainly:

The **subject** is the theme — stated *alone* by the first voice, which is why fugues begin with the most exposed moment in music: one naked line, whose profile you must remember for the next five minutes. Subjects are therefore built for *identity*: [a four-note cross](../works/cothen/bwv-849.md), [a hammered repeated note](../works/leipzig/bwv-885.md), [a leap and a silence](../works/cothen/bwv-867.md), [six full bars of paragraph](../works/leipzig/bwv-879.md) — anything, so long as you'll know it when it returns.

The **answer** is the second voice's entry: the subject transposed to the dominant (the key a fifth up — harmony's natural counterweight). If it's transposed exactly, it's a **real answer** ([I/9's textbook case](../works/cothen/bwv-854.md)); usually a note or two must bend to keep the harmony coherent — a **tonal answer**. While the second voice answers, the first continues with new material; if that continuation recurs faithfully against later entries, it's a **countersubject** — the subject's standing companion (and some fine fugues, [like I/6](../works/cothen/bwv-851.md), decline to keep one). When all voices have entered, the **exposition** is over — the only part of "fugue form" that's actually standardized.

After that: alternation. **Episodes** — connective passages developing fragments of the subject, usually in **sequence** (the same idea restated stepwise higher or lower, harmony traveling) — lead to fresh **entries** of the subject in related keys. The proportion of episode to entry *is* the fugue's character: entry-dense fugues feel monumental, episode-rich ones conversational.

Then the special devices, deployed when wanted — never obligatory. **Stretto**: entries overlapping before the subject finishes — the discussion becoming excited, everyone talking at once (climactic in [I/1](../works/cothen/bwv-846.md); structural at [II/5's four-voice pile-up](../works/leipzig/bwv-874.md)). **Inversion**: the subject upside down ([I/6](../works/cothen/bwv-851.md); the drama of [II/22](../works/leipzig/bwv-891.md) is upright-versus-inverted combat). **Augmentation**: the subject at doubled note-values — time slowed, gravity doubled ([II/2's late entrance](../works/leipzig/bwv-871.md); [I/8's learned grief](../works/cothen/bwv-853.md)). **Diminution**: halved values, the subject quickened. **Pedal point**: the bass freezing on one note while harmonies churn above — the arrival platform ([I/20 ends over one two hands can't even hold](../works/cothen/bwv-865.md)). And the multi-subject species: **double** and **triple fugues**, where two or three subjects get separate expositions and are then **combined** — the summit gesture of [II/14](../works/leipzig/bwv-883.md), [the St. Anne](../works/leipzig/bwv-552.md), and the unfinished last page of [the Art of Fugue](../works/leipzig/art-of-fugue.md).

Now the corrective examples, because Bach's greatness here is *range*, not rigor alone. A fugue can be a dance ([the passepied of I/11](../works/cothen/bwv-856.md), [the gigue of II/4](../works/leipzig/bwv-873.md), [the gavotte with a trill for a hat, II/13](../works/leipzig/bwv-882.md)); a costume drama ([the French-overture fugue, I/5](../works/cothen/bwv-850.md)); a joke with a stumble for a punchline ([II/11](../works/leipzig/bwv-880.md)); a two-voice sprint ([I/10](../works/cothen/bwv-855.md)); or a five-voice liturgy ([I/4](../works/cothen/bwv-849.md)). It can renounce every device and live on charm ([I/3](../works/cothen/bwv-848.md), [I/13](../works/cothen/bwv-858.md)) — proof the devices are vocabulary, not virtue. And it can scale from keyboard miniature to [orchestra](../works/cothen/bwv-1047.md), [unaccompanied violin](../works/cothen/bwv-1005.md), and [double choir](../works/leipzig/bwv-225.md): fugue is a property of *thought*, indifferent to medium — this timeline's most repeated lesson.

How to study one — a practical liturgy, usable on any of the 48: **(1)** Learn the subject cold: sing it, transpose it, know its skeleton. **(2)** Count the voices and mark every entry — pencil in the score or ears on the recording; the [verified reference table](../reference/wtc-verified-facts.md) tells you how many speakers to listen for. **(3)** Find the episodes and name what fragment each develops. **(4)** Locate the *one* structural event the fugue is built toward — a stretto, a combination, a pedal, a silence. Every Bach fugue has one; finding it is finding the piece. **(5)** Only then ask the expressive question — and notice you can now answer it in evidence rather than adjectives.

The discipline's deepest teaching is the one [the first essay](craft-01-counterpoint.md) promised: in a fugue, *law and freedom stop being opposites*. The subject constrains everything; the constraint is why the freedom means something. Bach spent his whole life saying that — in [a teaching book](craft-02-invention.md), in [a king's parlor](../events/leipzig-3/potsdam-1747.md), and finally [with his own name stitched into the unfinished summit](../works/leipzig/art-of-fugue.md) — and the fugue was how he said it most completely.
