Every thread in this Craft section — invention, fugue, canon, chorale, theology — converges on the same last decade, and on a single strange fact: **in his final ten years, with no commission, no deadline, and a musical culture that had moved on, Bach systematically wrote the complete book on his own art.** Not memoirs; *demonstrations*. One per domain, each exhaustive: variation ([the Goldbergs](../works/leipzig/goldberg-variations.md), 1741), the tonal universe revisited ([WTC II](../works/leipzig/wtc-2.md), ~1742), fugue and canon on a given theme ([the Musical Offering](../works/leipzig/musical-offering.md), 1747), strict canon on a chorale ([the Canonic Variations](../works/leipzig/bwv-769.md), 1747), the church's whole musical history ([the B minor Mass](../works/leipzig/b-minor-mass.md), 1748–49), and fugue as such ([the Art of Fugue](../works/leipzig/art-of-fugue.md), left one page short). This essay asks the question the shelf itself poses: *what was he doing?*

First, notice the shape these works share, because it's a **method**, and it had been his since childhood: take one small thing and exhaust it. A thirty-two-note bass; twenty-four keys; one royal theme; one carol; one D-minor subject. The [Inventions' title-page promise](craft-02-invention.md) — find a good idea and develop it *well* — executed at terminal scale. The late works add only totality: not "develop it well" but *develop it completely*, so that the piece becomes a survey of its own possibility-space. Encyclopedism was the family temperament ([the planned-164 Orgelbüchlein grid](../works/weimar/orgelbuechlein.md) at thirty; [six-of-everything at Cöthen](../eras/cothen.md)); the late decade is that temperament with nothing left to interrupt it.

Second, notice what the works answer. [Scheibe's 1737 verdict](../events/leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md) — turgid, unnatural, obsolete — was the fashionable consensus, and Bach never rebutted it in prose after Birnbaum's pamphlets. The late shelf *is* the rebuttal: counterpoint's case stated not defensively but exhaustively, in engraved publications ([mostly self-financed](../events/leipzig-2/clavier-ubung.md)) addressed to the only jury that could overrule his century — practitioners not yet born. This is not romantic projection; the *format* says it. These are teaching editions, systematically ordered, of precisely the disciplines his era called dead. You don't publish the complete grammar of a language you believe is dying unless you believe in resurrection — or at least in libraries.

Third, notice who the works are *for*, because the answer keeps widening. The Goldbergs address connoisseurs ("for the soul's delight of music-lovers," says the title page); the Offering, a king; the Canonic Variations, [a scientific society](../events/leipzig-3/mizler-1747.md); the Mass — no one alive: a Latin summa [no Leipzig liturgy could seat](../events/leipzig-3/b-minor-mass-1749.md), assembled from his life's best sacred music as if for an archive of the art itself. And the Art of Fugue's audience is, finally, **the discipline as such** — one theme interrogated until the interrogator's own name enters as a subject (B–A–C–H, the letters as notes) and the manuscript stops. Whether or not [the deathbed framing is legend](../events/leipzig-3/death-1750.md) (it partly is), the trajectory is documentary: each summa is addressed one step further past the market, until the addressee is posterity outright.

Resist, one last time, the two available sentimentalities. The bitter version — neglected genius, scorned by his time, writing for the drawer — founders on the facts: these were *publications*, sold, engraved at real cost, by a man [with a royal title](../events/leipzig-2/dresden-title-1733-36.md), famous sons, and full employment. The serene version — the craftsman above it all — founders on [the Erdmann letter](../people/georg-erdmann.md) and the pamphlet war: he felt the era's verdict, and it stung. The truer picture is stranger: a working professional who had answered every occasion for forty years — [wedding, funeral](../events/leipzig-1/cantors-household.md), [council election](../works/muehlhausen/bwv-71.md), [coffee house](../events/leipzig-2/collegium-1729.md), [king's parlor](../events/leipzig-3/potsdam-1747.md) — spending his last strength answering **the occasion behind the occasions**: the art itself, treated as the final patron, with the same punctual thoroughness he'd given every other client. *Soli Deo Gloria*, he still wrote at the ends — and if [essay six](craft-06-theology.md) is right that order cohering into harmony was his experience of the created world, then the late summa is simply the largest S.D.G. he could construct.

What happened to the completed book is [the next arc's story](../eras/eclipse.md) — the plates nearly sold for copper, the thirty subscribers, the long silence. But hold the image the summa leaves: the last page of the Art of Fugue, three subjects sounding, the fourth — the one that would bind them — implied by the design and absent from the paper. Every student who has ever traced that final page has felt the same thing: not incompleteness, but *assignment*. The complete book ends, deliberately or not, with an exercise left for the reader. This timeline is one very long attempt at it.
