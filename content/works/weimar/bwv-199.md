**What it is.** The great solo-soprano cantata: one voice's journey from self-loathing ("my heart swims in blood") to restored joy, in the most operatic sacred music of the Weimar years — a psychological monodrama in eight movements that Bach carried with him and revived at [each subsequent post](../../events/cothen/the-cothen-capelle.md).

**Listen for.** The opening recitative's raw immediacy — confession without formality; the central aria *Tief gebückt* ("deeply bowed"), long-arched contrition over full strings; and the turn: a chorale verse sung over a busy **viola** obbligato (the ensemble's humble middle voice carrying the consolation — a deliberate, touching casting choice), before the final gigue-aria dances the forgiven soul out.

**Why it matters.** The cantata-as-inner-drama at its purest — and, via Lorraine Hunt Lieberson's celebrated performances ([paired with BWV 82](../leipzig/bwv-82.md)), one of the works through which modern audiences rediscovered what these pieces do to a room. Arc III will return to that recording.
