**What it is.** The Weimar prelude-and-fugue ideal at full maturity: a prelude of serpentine chromatic figuration that keeps tightening its own spiral, and a long gigue-rhythm fugue whose subject dances for pages before the work detonates into a *stylus phantasticus* cadenza-coda — the north-German fire ([Buxtehude](../../people/dieterich-buxtehude.md)) now running through Italian structural discipline ([the Vivaldi conversion](../../events/weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md), audible as form).

**Listen for.** The pedal solo moments — Weimar-Bach's signature flex (contemporaries marveled most at his *feet*) — and the fugue's long paragraph-building: episodes that sequence outward, subject entries that land like pillars, the ending that abandons counterpoint entirely for sheer improvisatory weather. The two-stage form (free prelude, strict fugue, free coda) is the mature print of what [BWV 565](../arnstadt/bwv-565.md) sketches in crayon.

**Craft.** The pairing-of-opposites principle — fantasy then law — that governs [the WTC's whole design](../cothen/wtc-1.md), here at organ scale.

**Why it matters.** Liszt transcribed it for piano and made it a Romantic warhorse — one of the main channels by which the organ works reached the nineteenth-century concert public ([Arc II's](../../eras/eclipse.md) keyboard-first Bach again). For students: the single best Weimar prelude-and-fugue to know whole.
