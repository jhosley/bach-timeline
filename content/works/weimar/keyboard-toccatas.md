**What it is.** Young Bach unchained: seven multi-section toccatas in the north-German *stylus phantasticus* ([Buxtehude's manner](../../people/dieterich-buxtehude.md), harpsichord-sized) — free flourishes, brooding slow sections, and fugues (often two per toccata) strung into improvisatory sequences: the closest the keyboard works come to a transcript of the young virtuoso *playing at you*.

**Listen for.** The D-major (BWV 912) — the extrovert: swagger, a hymn-like adagio, a gigue-fugue ending in mock-tragic theatrics; the F-sharp minor (910) and C minor (911) — the depth pair, their fugues already unmistakably *him*; the E-minor (914), compact enough to be every student's first.

**Craft.** The before-photograph: sectional fantasy *preceding* the Italian formal discipline ([Weimar's conversion](../../events/weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md)) — hear these, then [the English Suite preludes](../cothen/english-suites.md), and the arrival of *architecture* in Bach's keyboard music is audible as a life event. Several survive via [the Ohrdruf brother's manuscript collections](../../people/johann-christoph-bach-ohrdruf.md) — the guardian archiving the ward.

**Why it matters.** The wild youth the mature monuments grew from — kept in the catalog to make the growth visible.
