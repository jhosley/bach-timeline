**What it is.** The inaugural work of [the Konzertmeister duty](../../events/weimar/konzertmeister-1714.md): Palm Sunday 1714, the King of Heaven welcomed into Jerusalem — and Bach welcomed into regular cantata production — in one gesture. Chamber-scaled for [the Himmelsburg gallery](../../events/weimar/himmelsburg.md): solo recorder, divided violas, intimacy throughout.

**Listen for.** The opening sonata: a processional in dotted French-overture rhythm — recorder and pizzicato-like violin painting the donkey-gait entry into the city, royalty at walking pace. Then the new Italian machinery arriving in Bach's church music in real time: ritornello choruses, da capo arias (the alto's *Leget euch dem Heiland unter* the jewel). The penultimate chorale fantasia weaves *Jesu, deine Passion* — Palm Sunday already looking toward Good Friday, the liturgical long game Bach never stopped playing.

**Why it matters.** A dated hinge: church cantata, before and after, [split on this Sunday](../../events/weimar/konzertmeister-1714.md). Study it beside [BWV 4](../muehlhausen/bwv-4.md) — seven years apart, different species — and the Weimar synthesis (Italy's forms, Germany's chorales, France's rhythms) is audible as a before/after photograph.
