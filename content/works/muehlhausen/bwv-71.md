**What it is.** Civic splendor by a 22-year-old: psalm verses and hymn for the town council's inauguration, deployed as **four spatially separated instrumental-vocal choirs** in the Venetian manner — brass here, recorders there, the church itself made into the orchestra. Styled *motetto* on its title page; [the only cantata printed in Bach's lifetime](../../events/muehlhausen/bwv71-printed-1708.md).

**Listen for.** The spatial games — blocks of sound answering across the room (choose a recording with real separation) — and, at the center, the quiet coup: *Du wollest dem Feinde nicht geben*, a subdued, chromatic prayer entirely out of scale with the civic pomp around it, the young composer smuggling inwardness into a state occasion. The aria for the retiring 83-year-old burgomaster (*Tag und Nacht ist dein*) is occasional music with actual tenderness.

**Craft.** Bach's one surviving essay in the grand polychoral tradition (Gabrieli's lineage via Schütz) — a style he then abandoned; the double-choir [Matthew Passion](../leipzig/st-matthew-passion.md) and [motets](../leipzig/motets.md) are its distant heirs.

**Why it matters.** The printed parts made this, for two centuries, the *only* Bach cantata the world could easily know — Arc II's economics ([see the event](../../events/muehlhausen/bwv71-printed-1708.md)) in one artifact.
