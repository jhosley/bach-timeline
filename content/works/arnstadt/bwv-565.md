**What it is.** The most famous organ piece on earth — the mordent-and-plunge opening that means "organ" (and, unfairly, "horror movie") to the whole planet — and, deliciously, the least secure item in the whole catalog: no autograph, one chain of late copies, stylistic oddities everywhere (unison passages, simplistic harmonies, a fugue that keeps abandoning its own rules), leading serious scholars to propose it's very early Bach, arranged Bach, or not Bach.

**Listen for.** With the doubts in mind, hear it fresh: the *stylus phantasticus* ([Buxtehude's theatrical manner](../../people/dieterich-buxtehude.md)) at maximum voltage and minimum discipline — rhetorical flourishes, dramatic silences, an improviser's structure. If it *is* the young Arnstadt Bach, it's the sound of [the organist the consistory complained about](../../events/arnstadt/consistory-rebukes-1706.md): all lightning, learning still arriving.

**Why it matters.** Twice over: as the great **provenance object-lesson** — the world's favorite "Bach" is the catalog's shakiest attribution, the *Documented/Tradition/Disputed* system earning its keep — and as reception history: its fame is a *twentieth-century* artifact (Stokowski's orchestration, Disney's *Fantasia*, 1940), a case study for Arc III in how mass media, not manuscripts, crowned Bach's pop-culture emblem.
