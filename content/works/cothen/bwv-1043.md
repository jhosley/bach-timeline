**What it is.** The most beloved of Bach's concertos, and structurally the most contrapuntal: two equal violins whose entire relationship is imitation — each idea stated by one, answered by the other, their lines constantly crossing so the ear loses track of who is leading. The concerto as a portrait of friendship, or rivalry, or both (one imagines Spiess and a colleague at Cöthen, or Bach's students at the Leipzig coffee house).

**Listen for.** The Largo — perhaps the most purely loved slow movement Bach wrote: a long cantilena in F major where the second violin's answer overlaps the first's phrase *before it finishes*, so the melody seems never to breathe and never to end. This is **imitative counterpoint doing emotional work** — technique and tenderness indistinguishable, the whole Craft section's thesis in four minutes.

**Craft.** The outer movements are fugal expositions embedded in ritornello form; the canon essay uses the Largo's overlapping entries as the example that imitation is expressive, not academic. Later arranged by Bach for two harpsichords (BWV 1062).
