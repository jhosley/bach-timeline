**What it is.** The first of the three suite sets and the biggest-boned: unlike [the French Suites](french-suites.md), each opens with a substantial **Prelude** — several of them full concerto movements in keyboard dress, ritornello engines running ([Weimar's Italian conversion](../../events/weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md), fresh in the tank) before the French dances begin. Italy at the door, France in the house.

**Listen for.** No. 2 in A minor and No. 3 in G minor — the recital favorites: driving preludes, sarabandes of veiled grief (both with written-out ornamented repeats — Bach teaching *how* to vary a repeat), and whipcrack gigues. No. 6 in D minor is the summa: a prelude on concerto scale and a gigue whose trill-torn subject borders on the demonic.

**Craft.** The three-sets comparison (English → French → [Partitas](../leipzig/partitas.md)) is the best packaged tour of Bach's stylistic development in one genre: same dances, a decade and a half of deepening. Start it here.

**Why it matters.** Where Bach first fused his two imported languages *at length* — and proof (against the "French Suites are for students, English for players" cliché) that the suite was serious architecture for him from the start.
