**What it is.** Twenty-four preludes and twenty-four fugues, one pair in every major and minor key, ascending chromatically from C major to B minor. Each **prelude** is a free character piece — an arpeggio study, an aria, a two-part invention, a French overture; each **fugue** is a strict demonstration of imitative counterpoint in two to five voices. The pairing is the point: fantasy and law, freedom and rigor, on facing pages twenty-four times.

**Listen for.** Treat it as a gallery, not a sequence. First visit: C major's prelude (pure arpeggiated harmony — the keyboard twin of the first cello suite's prelude); C-sharp major (a key that had barely *existed* before well-tempering); E-flat minor's grief-laden prelude and its austere fugue; the B-minor fugue whose chromatic subject contains all twelve tones — the book ending at the edge of the tonal world it just mapped.

**Craft.** The central text of the fugue essay and the temperament essay both. Individual cards for all 48 pairs arrive in phase R3; this pilot card holds the set's place.

**Reception thread.** Unpublished until 1801 — yet the most influential "book" in keyboard history, circulating for 80 years in hand copies. Mozart arranged its fugues for strings; Beethoven played it whole as a boy; Chopin's Préludes answer it; Shostakovich's op. 87 answers it again. When Arc II's ribbon dims, this is the brightest thread still glowing.
