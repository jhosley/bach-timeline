**What it is.** The set's deliberate awkward child: E-flat major offers the cello almost no open strings, so nothing rings for free — every sonority must be earned by the left hand. The reward is a veiled, burnished tone unlike any other suite. Galanterie pair: two Bourrées.

**Listen for.** The Prelude's stubborn design: unbroken chains of wide-spanning eighth-note arpeggios, interrupted twice by sudden free cadenza-like flourishes — architecture and improvisation trading places. The Sarabande walks with unusual dotted formality, closer to courtly ceremony than lament.

**Craft.** Proof that Bach chose keys *structurally*: across the six suites he surveys easy-ringing keys and resistant ones as deliberately as the Brandenburgs survey ensembles. For a student, the E-flat suite is where you feel the instrument's physics as part of the composition itself.
