**What it is.** The set's first tragedy — D minor, the Chaconne's key, and a similar gravity in miniature. Galanterie pair: two Minuets (the first minor, the second a brief major-mode consolation).

**Listen for.** The Prelude behaves like an accompanied recitative with the accompaniment removed: speech-like phrases, questioning silences, and a famous ending — three bare chords where every performer must decide how much to break, roll, or sustain. The Sarabande is the suite's heart, its melody constantly sinking back onto dissonant appoggiaturas (leaning-notes that ache before resolving — the Baroque's basic unit of expressive pain).

**Craft.** A study in how much *rhetoric* — pause, question, emphasis — a single line can carry. Gardiner's usage is apt here: Bach's instrumental lines "speak." Pair with the D-minor keyboard works for the key's family resemblance.
