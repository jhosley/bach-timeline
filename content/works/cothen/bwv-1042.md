**What it is.** The sunnier twin of the A-minor concerto, opening with a three-note E-major hammer-stroke that behaves like a motto for the whole movement.

**Listen for.** The first movement is a **da capo** structure (A–B–A, the opera aria's form) imposed on a concerto — the outer sections in radiant major, the central one turning through minor keys and quiet suspense before the opening returns verbatim. The slow movement is another ground-bass aria: the bass intones its solemn figure over and over while the violin mourns above in one of Bach's longest sustained melodies. The finale, a rondeau, alternates its dance refrain with progressively showier solo couplets.

**Craft.** Aria form, ground bass, rondeau: three vocal and dance archetypes wearing concerto dress — a compact illustration that Bach's instrumental forms are social forms (opera, church lament, courtly dance) abstracted. Later rebuilt as harpsichord concerto BWV 1054.
