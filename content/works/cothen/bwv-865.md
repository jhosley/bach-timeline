**The pair.** Book I's contrapuntal marathon: after a driving prelude, the longest fugue of the book — **four voices** hammering a sturdy subject through inversions and strettos with almost organ-like relentlessness, and ending famously **over a pedal point that two hands alone cannot fully sustain** — evidence (much debated) of organ thinking, or a pedal-harpsichord, inside the "48."

**Study.** The endurance fugue: chart its long middle for where tension is *maintained* rather than raised — the art of the plateau. And the ending's practical puzzle is a healthy reminder: these pieces predate our instrument certainties.
