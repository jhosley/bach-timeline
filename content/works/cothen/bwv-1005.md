**What it is.** The summit of the three sonatas: Adagio — Fuga — Largo — Allegro assai, with a Fuga of over 350 bars — the longest fugue Bach composed for *any* instrument, keyboard and organ included, entrusted to the instrument least equipped to play one.

**Listen for.** The fugue's subject is drawn from the Pentecost chorale *Komm, Heiliger Geist* ("Come, Holy Spirit") — a hymn Bach's audience would have known — and among its adventures is a full section in **inversion** (the subject turned upside down, every rise becoming a fall). After that immensity, the Largo is disarming simplicity: a two-voice song, and the finale pure release.

**Craft.** A chorale-derived subject, stretto, inversion, and episodic architecture all on four strings: this fugue is the *Sei Solo*'s claim that counterpoint is a property of *thought*, not of instruments. The fugue craft essay pairs it with keyboard fugues on similar subjects to show what changes — and what doesn't — when the medium fights back.
