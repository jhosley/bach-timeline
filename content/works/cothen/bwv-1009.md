**What it is.** The extrovert of the six — C major exploiting the cello's two lowest open strings (C and G) so the instrument rings like a small orchestra. Galanterie pair: two Bourrées, the first of which is the suite's pop hit.

**Listen for.** The Prelude's grand scale-and-arpeggio rhetoric, ending in a page of pealing chords over a repeated bass pedal — the cello imitating an organ's full registration. In the Bourrée pair, the contrast trick the whole set uses: a bright C-major dance, its C-minor twin shadowed and inward, then the return of the first — ABA by juxtaposition.

**Craft.** Open strings as architecture: Bach designs the figuration so the resonant open C and G keep sounding under changing harmonies — instrument-specific craft that transcription (piano, guitar) flattens. Useful study case for why *idiomatic* writing and *pure* counterpoint aren't opposites in Bach.
