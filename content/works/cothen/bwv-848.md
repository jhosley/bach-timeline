**The pair.** Seven sharps — a key that had barely *existed* as playable territory before well-tempering, and Bach plants a flag of pure delight in it: a glittering, feather-light prelude and a **three-voice fugue** notable for what it omits — no stretto, no inversion, no learned devices at all: wit and momentum are the whole argument.

**Study.** The temperament essay's exhibit A: this music is *about* its own key's novelty. Play (or imagine) it a half-step down in C — perfectly possible, and somehow entirely wrong: key-color is real, and this pair proves it.
