**What it is.** For many scholars ([Gardiner](../../people/john-eliot-gardiner.md) among them) the first-cycle summit and one of the greatest cantatas outright: "Lord, do not enter into judgment with your servant" — guilt, trembling, and assurance composed with devices decades ahead of their time.

**Listen for.** The soprano aria *Wie zittern und wanken* — the guilty conscience — scored **without any continuo**: no bass line at all, the harmonic floor removed, the soul audibly *unsupported* while an oboe circles and the strings shudder. It is the most famous single orchestration decision in the cantatas — anxiety as physics. Then its answer: the bass's arioso over steadily calming strings, and the final chorale, whose accompanying tremors **slow, movement by phrase, into stillness** as the text moves from terror to peace — the orchestra itself being pacified.

**Why it matters.** Week nine of the job, and Bach is already dismantling the genre's conventions for expressive ends. If a skeptic asks why the cantatas matter, play this one.
