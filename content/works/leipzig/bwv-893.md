**The pair.** The quiet exit — and the deliberate anti-rhyme with [Book I's ending](../cothen/bwv-869.md): where 1722 closed the tonal universe with a Largo containing all twelve notes, 1742 closes with a *dancing* two-part prelude and a light, folk-touched **three-voice fugue** that simply — after ninety-six pieces across two books — bows out with a **Picardy-third** smile.

**Study.** Endings as self-portraits: the young encyclopedist ends in cosmic grief; the old master, having built [the Mass](b-minor-mass.md) and begun [the Art of Fugue](art-of-fugue.md) in this very key-world, ends the "48" with a dance and a major chord. Read the difference however you like — the timeline only insists you notice it.
