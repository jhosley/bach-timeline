**What it is.** Six (by traditional count) unaccompanied-style choral works — the old Lutheran **motet** genre, scripture and chorale woven for voices alone (continuo and doubling instruments assumed in practice) — written mostly for **funerals**: the occasions where Leipzig ordered its grandest choral craftsmanship. This is the genre of [the Lüneburg library's](../../events/lueneburg/michaelis-library.md) old masters, and Bach wrote them as the tradition's conscious heir — and terminus.

**Listen for.** *Singet dem Herrn ein neues Lied* (BWV 225): two four-part choirs hurling jubilation across the aisle — the work [Mozart heard in 1789](../../eras/eclipse.md) and demanded the parts of, exclaiming "now, there is something one can learn from!" *Jesu, meine Freude* (BWV 227): eleven movements in a perfect symmetrical arch, chorale verses alternating with scripture — the funeral sermon as architecture. *Komm, Jesu, komm*: death as homecoming, in the tenderest double-choir writing of the century.

**Craft.** The counterpoint essays' choral anchor: fugue, chorale-fantasia, and antiphony with no orchestra to hide behind — the choir *is* the whole machine.

**Reception thread.** The one vocal corpus that **never went dark**: the Thomanerchor sang the motets continuously from Bach's death to this day — the thread on which [the 1789 Mozart scene](../../eras/eclipse.md) hangs, and the proof that the eclipse was never quite total where the manuscripts stayed home. Individual cards for all six arrive with the R3 vocal completion batch.
