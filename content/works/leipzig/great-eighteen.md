**What it is.** In the 1740s, alongside [the Mass](b-minor-mass.md) and [the Art of Fugue](../../events/leipzig-3/art-of-fugue-begun.md), Bach fair-copied a legacy collection of his great **Weimar chorale fantasias, revised** — the large-scale settings the [Orgelbüchlein's](../weimar/orgelbuechlein.md) miniatures deliberately weren't: expansive, multi-verse, the hymn examined at cathedral length.

**Listen for.** *An Wasserflüssen Babylon* (BWV 653) — the very chorale [the teenager copied at Böhm's](../../events/lueneburg/boehm-tablatures.md) and [the master improvised on for Reincken](../../events/cothen/hamburg-reincken-1720.md), here fixed in final form: one melody, a whole life's thread. The two *Nun komm, der Heiden Heiland* settings — the ornamented one dissolving Advent's hymn into arabesque grief. *Schmücke dich* — Mendelssohn wept over it; Schumann reported it. And last in the manuscript, **BWV 668**, *Vor deinen Thron tret ich hiermit* ("Before thy throne I now appear") — [the deathbed-dictation legend's](../../events/leipzig-3/death-1750.md) object, entered in another hand when Bach's eyes failed: the collection ends, literally, at the throne.

**Craft.** The definitive revision corpus: Weimar originals survive for comparison — thirty years of second thoughts, visible note by note. The R4 essays mine it for what "better" meant to late Bach (almost always: barer).

**Why it matters.** The organ thread's true summa — youth's material, age's judgment, one manuscript — and the most moving *object* in the instrumental catalog.
