**What it is.** [The Potsdam evening](../../events/leipzig-3/potsdam-1747.md), monumentalized: everything a single chromatic theme — the *thema regium*, the king's theme — can become. Two **ricercars** (the archaic word for fugue, chosen pointedly): the three-voice one approximating what Bach improvised that night, and the **six-voice ricercar** he had declined to improvise — delivered in print, the grandest strict fugue he ever wrote for keyboard. Ten **canons**, several engraved as riddles (one line of music, a cryptic Latin motto — *solve it yourself, Majesty*): among them a canon that modulates up a whole tone each repetition, inscribed "as the notes ascend, so may the King's glory." And a four-movement **trio sonata** in the king's own beloved galant flute style — with the severe royal theme smuggled, again and again, through its fashionable textures.

**Listen for.** The six-voice ricercar above all: six real voices at keyboard, no seams showing — the summit of the mountain that began with [two-voice Inventions](../cothen/inventions-sinfonias.md). In the sonata, the double game: music Frederick would enjoy, built from material he set as a trap.

**Craft.** The R4 canon and riddle essays live here; so does the wit essay — the whole *Offering* is courtesy with an edge.

**Reception thread.** Webern's 1935 orchestration of the six-voice ricercar — the melody passed instrument to instrument, tone color made structure — is Arc III's modernist Bach in one gesture.
