**What it is.** The showpiece: solo soprano and solo trumpet in open competition — "Exult in God in every land!" — Bach's most unabashedly virtuosic sacred work, its outer movements a duel of high Cs and its label (*for any time*) admitting it was built to travel.

**Listen for.** The opening's paired fireworks — voice and clarino trading fanfares as equals ([the Second Brandenburg's](../cothen/bwv-1047.md) balancing act, reduced to two champions); the central aria's sudden intimacy (the continuo alone, the voice conversing with its own bass line); and the finale: a chorale verse that *erupts* into a concluding **"Alleluja" fugue** for voice and trumpet — doxology as athletics.

**The mystery.** Who sang it? Leipzig's church sopranos were schoolboys; this part wants a professional's stamina and range. A visiting Dresden virtuosa? An exceptional Thomaner? The sources are silent — a live scholarly puzzle the card leaves open, and a pointed footnote to [the 1730 memo's](../../events/leipzig-2/memo-1730.md) complaints, written *weeks before this piece*: whatever he said about his forces, that September he had someone extraordinary.

**Why it matters.** Proof against every stereotype of Leipzig-Bach as austere: joy, display, and spiritual seriousness in one ten-minute frame — and the modern soprano repertoire's favorite Bach audition.
