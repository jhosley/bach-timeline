**What it is.** The debut: Bach's first Sunday as cantor, and he delivered a **fourteen-movement, two-part manifesto** — "The wretched shall eat," riches versus poverty across both halves of the sermon — at roughly double a normal cantata's length. A local chronicler noted the new cantor's first music "with great applause"; Bach was announcing scale.

**Listen for.** The opening chorus behaving like a French overture in disguise (grave dotted opening, fugal continuation — the new man showing the full toolkit immediately); and the structural signature: each part ends with the **same chorale fantasia** on *Was Gott tut, das ist wohlgetan*, the hymn's tune ringing in a trumpet-led orchestral frame — the congregational tune as the work's twin pillars.

**Craft.** The two-part, sermon-straddling design and the chorale-frame architecture are the *Leipzig format* being minted in real time; [BWV 76, the following Sunday, matches it exactly](../../events/leipzig-1/cantata-machine.md) — a paired opening statement, planned as such.

**Why it matters.** Day one of [the machine](../../events/leipzig-1/cantata-machine.md). Hear it as the congregation did — the new cantor's first word — and the ambition is unmistakable: this was not a man planning to coast on a third-choice appointment.
