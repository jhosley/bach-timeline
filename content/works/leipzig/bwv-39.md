**What it is.** The social-conscience cantata: Isaiah's "Break your bread for the hungry" in the greatest of the third-cycle choruses — a vast, compassionate design whose opening gestures are the text made physical: short, broken figures passed among recorders and oboes — **bread being broken** — before the choir's long lines gather the fragments into charity.

**Listen for.** That opening's three linked sections (breaking — sharing — the promise of light); the alto aria's warmth; the bass's Hebrews verse set as arioso — scripture given the Vox Christi register without a Gospel narrative.

**Why it matters.** Proof the machine's later, slower years (post-1725) produced summits too — and a provenance lesson in the dating note: the beloved refugee story dies on the calendar, per this timeline's rules. What remains is bigger: Leipzig's charity Sundays, and Bach composing *ethics* with the same tools he gave to doctrine.
