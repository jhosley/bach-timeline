**What it is.** The complete Latin Mass Ordinary at maximal scale — two hours, twenty-seven movements — assembled in Bach's last working years from the finest sacred music of his whole life, re-texted and perfected ([the event card](../../events/leipzig-3/b-minor-mass-1749.md) tells the biographical story). Not written for any service he could attend; never heard whole in his lifetime.

**Listen for.** The opening: a five-bar B-minor cry of *Kyrie* — then the vast fugue that spends eleven minutes earning it. The **architecture of styles**: Renaissance *stile antico* (Credo's opening, *Confiteor*) beside high-Baroque concerto choruses (*Gloria*, *Et resurrexit*) beside the most modern galant duets — a deliberate museum of sacred music's whole history, curated by its master. The **Crucifixus**: a chaconne on a grinding chromatic ground ([born in Weimar, 1714](../weimar/bwv-12.md)), sinking at its end into burial's E minor — out of which *Et resurrexit* detonates without a breath. And the ending: *Dona nobis pacem* re-uses the *Gratias* fugue — the Mass closes by *giving thanks and asking peace with the same music*, the parody principle elevated into theology.

**Craft.** Every R4 essay lands here eventually: fugue, canon (*Confiteor*'s chant-in-canon), chorale-thinking without chorales, parody as transfiguration, number and symmetry (the debates dealt with soberly).

**Reception thread.** Emanuel performed the Credo in 1786; the nineteenth century built it into "the greatest work of music of all ages and all peoples" (the Swiss publisher Nägeli, announcing the first edition); today it is the work by which everything else — Bach's included — gets measured.
