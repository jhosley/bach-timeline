**What it is.** Matthew's Passion narrative — Evangelist, Christ, the crowd — interleaved with Picander's contemplative arias and with chorales the congregation knew by heart, built for **two complete choirs and orchestras** in spatial dialogue. Christ's words alone are haloed by sustained strings — a radiance that vanishes exactly once, at *Eli, Eli, lama asabthani*. Roughly three hours: the largest musical structure of Bach's life.

**Listen for.** The double-choir architecture as *theology staged in space* — Zion questioning, the Faithful answering, from the first chorus's *Sehet! — Wen?* exchanges onward. The arias as stations of private conscience: *Buß und Reu*, *Erbarme dich* (alto and solo violin, after Peter's denial — perhaps the most merciful page in music), *Mache dich, mein Herze, rein*. And the *O Haupt voll Blut und Wunden* chorale returning five times, harmonized darker each time — the congregation's own hymn made to carry the narrative's descending weight.

**Craft.** The R4 essays use it everywhere: chorale as structural rebar; recitative as speech-made-pitch; the da capo aria bent to liturgical purpose; key-architecture across three hours.

**Reception thread.** Performed a handful of times under Bach, then silent for a century — until a twenty-year-old Mendelssohn [raised it in Berlin in 1829](../../eras/revival.md) and restarted Bach's fame. The work is the timeline's spine: it appears in Arc I as achievement, Arc II as resurrection vehicle, Arc III as the repertoire's Everest.
