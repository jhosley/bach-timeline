**What it is.** The concentrated one: three compact movements, F-minor seriousness at chamber scale — famous almost entirely for its centerpiece.

**Listen for.** The **Largo**: over gently plucked strings, the keyboard sings one long, arching cantilena — music of such untroubled serenity that it has escaped its container completely (film scores, weddings, a thousand "Arioso" arrangements). Bach used the same melody as a cantata sinfonia for oboe (*Ich steh mit einem Fuß im Grabe* — "I stand with one foot in the grave": the serenity is *deathbed* serenity, a context its easy-listening afterlife has entirely shed — the timeline restores the label). The outer movements frame it with terse, syncopation-flecked F-minor argument.

**Why it matters.** The catalog's cleanest example of **context-stripping in reception**: the same notes meaning "peaceful" to moderns and "ready to die well" to Leipzig. Which hearing is right is a question worth carrying through your whole study of this composer.
