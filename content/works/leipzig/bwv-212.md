**What it is.** "We've got a new squire!" — in Saxon **dialect**: a village couple celebrates (and needles) the new lord of a manor near Leipzig, in Bach's most down-to-earth score: an overture that's a medley of rustic dance tunes, arias built on folk melodies and popular songs, drone basses, a tavern-band flute-and-fiddle world — the learned cantor, at fifty-seven, writing barn music *well*, with evident delight.

**Listen for.** The overture's deliberate patchwork (dances butting into each other mid-phrase — a village band's set list); the couple's flirt-and-grumble duets; the wink where the music briefly goes "high style" for the flattery aria — and drops it again.

**Why it matters.** Written [the year after the Goldbergs](../../events/leipzig-3/goldberg-1741.md): hold the two side by side — strict canon cosmology and dialect comedy, twelve months apart — and every remaining stereotype of Bach dissolves. The completest composer who ever lived, at both ends of the register, on his way into [the late summa years](../../eras/leipzig-3.md).
