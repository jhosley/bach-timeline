**What it is.** The summit of the free organ works — Leipzig-Bach's answer to his own Weimar decade: bigger, stricter, more symphonic. The nickname is the fugue subject's shape: from a repeated central note it opens **outward in widening leaps** — a wedge drawn in sound, austere as a diagram and unforgettable as a face.

**Listen for.** The prelude as proto-symphony: a brooding ritornello structure ([the Italian engine](../../events/weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md), fully Germanized) whose material develops with an intensity pointing straight at later centuries — Bach's grandest single organ movement. The fugue's shock is *formal*: its middle turns into a toccata — episodes of free, whirling figuration invading the strict form — before the fugue's opening section returns **note for note**: a **da capo fugue**, opera's ABA imposed on counterpoint's cathedral. Law, fantasy, law.

**Craft.** The R4 fugue essay's late-style exhibit: fugue no longer as texture but as *dramatic architecture* — form itself the expressive event. Compare [the Wedge's](bwv-548.md) return-as-triumph with [the WTC's](../cothen/wtc-1.md) miniature closures.

**Why it matters.** Where the organ thread culminates before [the late chorale summas](great-eighteen.md): the instrument of Bach's youth, mastered past anyone's imagining, one last time at full civic scale.
