**What it is.** The gates of [Clavier-Übung III](../../events/leipzig-2/clavier-ubung.md): a French-overture prelude opening the volume, a triple fugue closing it, with Lutheran doctrine (the catechism chorales) housed between — the whole conceived as one liturgical-architectural span in print.

**Listen for.** Threes everywhere, and meant: E-flat's **three flats**; the prelude's three distinct themes (royal dotted grandeur; an echoing galant duet; a driving fugato) circulating like persons of one substance; the fugue in **three sections on three subjects** — the first a stately hymn-like theme (its resemblance to the English hymn tune "St. Anne"/"O God, Our Help" gave the nickname; coincidence, almost certainly), each later section adding a new subject and finally **combining them**. The Trinity reading is old, obvious, and — for once — almost certainly intended: this is doctrine as design, published the year after [Scheibe called such art unnatural](../../events/leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md).

**Craft.** The R4 essays' cleanest case of **theological form**: not pictorialism (no birds, no waves) but *structure as confession*. And technically the gateway drug to [the Art of Fugue's](../../events/leipzig-3/art-of-fugue-begun.md) multi-subject engineering.

**Why it matters.** Bach's own chosen public monument for the organ — engraved, framed, sold under his name. The organ thread's official summa, as he curated it.
