**What it is.** Transience — "ah how fleeting, ah how trifling" is human life — composed as *motion that will not hold still*: the opening fantasia's scales rush up and vanish, figures chase and evaporate around the plain chorale line, life as mist audibly dispersing (the hymn's own image: a fog that rises and dissolves).

**Listen for.** The tenor aria — life as **rushing water**, flute and violin streaming in and out of each other's eddies, one of the cycle's virtuoso showpieces; then the bass aria's grim pomp ("earthly treasures are as flames consumed") with its stamping, hollow grandeur.

**Why it matters.** The chorale cycle's *vanitas* masterpiece and its best display of figure-as-theology: nothing in the piece merely accompanies — every scale, stream, and stamp is the sermon. Late-November church-year darkness (the run of "last things" Sundays) at maximum craft.
