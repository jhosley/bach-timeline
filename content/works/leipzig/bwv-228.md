**What it is.** Isaiah's double reassurance — "Fear not, I am with you" — as a two-stage design: first the double choirs trading the words of comfort antiphonally, the phrase "ich bin bei dir" (*I am with you*) passed back and forth like a hand held from both sides; then the choirs merge, and over a running fugal texture on "Ich habe dich bei deinem Namen gerufen" the sopranos float two verses of a Paul Gerhardt chorale — scripture below, hymn above, the motet's signature double decker.

**Listen for.** The pivot from dialogue to union — eight parts becoming one texture at exactly the words "I have called you by your name; you are mine": form enacting adoption. And everywhere the tenderness of key: A major, rare and radiant in the motets.

**Why it matters.** The intimate one — where [Singet](bwv-225.md) blazes and [Jesu, meine Freude](bwv-227.md) builds, this consoles person-to-person; funeral music addressed to *you*.
