**What it is.** The longest and most architectural motet: **eleven movements in a perfect mirror** — six verses of the hymn *Jesu, meine Freude* alternating with five verses of Romans 8, arranged symmetrically around a central fugue ("Ihr aber seid nicht fleischlich"). Hymn (comfort) and scripture (doctrine) interlocked, the whole a chiasm — the cross shape Baroque theology loved — in time.

**Listen for.** The frame: first and last movements sing the same plain chorale harmonization — you leave through the door you entered. Inside, the range: "Trotz dem alten Drachen" (*defiance* of the old dragon, the five voices actually snarling), the veiled trio "Denn das Gesetz," and the aching "Gute Nacht, o Wesen" — the world bidden goodnight in a lullaby of suspensions, the still center of the whole funeral rite.

**Why it matters.** The choral conductor's textbook of structure-as-consolation, and the clearest sung demonstration of the timeline's [chorale-as-classroom](../../events/eisenach/luthers-town.md) thread: eleven ways to inhabit one hymn.
