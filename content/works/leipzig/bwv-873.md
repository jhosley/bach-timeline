**The pair.** The key of [Book I's five-voice monument](../cothen/bwv-849.md) — answered, with late-Bach wit, by its opposite: an ornate, aria-like prelude of real pathos, then a **three-voice fugue in 12/16, in the style of an Italian gigue** — grief's old address reoccupied by dance.

**Study.** The two C-sharp minor fugues are the books' single most instructive cross-pair: same key, 1722's cathedral versus 1742's dance — not a change of depth but of *register* (the late gigue's minor-mode flight is anything but shallow). Whatever "late style" means, it's audible here.
