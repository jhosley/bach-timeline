**What it is.** A ten-movement comic miniature-opera for [Zimmermann's coffee house](../../events/leipzig-2/collegium-1729.md): grumpy father Schlendrian ("Slowcoach") tries to break daughter Lieschen's coffee habit by escalating threats — no walks, no dresses, finally *no husband* — and she capitulates... while secretly resolving to marry only a man who'll write coffee into the wedding contract. Leipzig laughing at its own caffeine craze (then a genuine moral controversy), *in the den itself*.

**Listen for.** Lieschen's aria ***Ei! wie schmeckt der Coffee süße*** — "ah, how sweet the coffee tastes, lovelier than a thousand kisses" — a flute-wreathed love song addressed to a beverage, and genuinely beautiful: Bach refuses to write down to comedy. Schlendrian's growling basso frustration (the continuo *stomping*); the recitatives' sitcom timing; the closing trio shrugging that coffee-drinking is hereditary anyway — "die Katze läßt das Mausen nicht" (cats will chase mice).

**Craft.** The same aria-and-recitative machinery as the church works, retuned for wit — the era's proof (with [the Peasant Cantata](../../events/leipzig-2/collegium-1729.md), its rustic sibling of 1742) that the "Fifth Evangelist" image is a nineteenth-century cropping of a fuller man.

**Why it matters.** Documented Bach humor — rare, precious, and performed today more than almost any secular cantata. Play it for anyone who thinks they don't like Bach; play it for anyone who thinks Bach didn't like *fun*.
