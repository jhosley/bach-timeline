**What it is.** The other supreme bass solo cantata, twin pillar to [BWV 82](bwv-82.md): life as a sea-voyage under the cross — "I will gladly carry the cross-staff" — carried by one singer from burden to arrival.

**Listen for.** The opening aria's freight: a laboring, syncopated line that *carries weight* — you can hear the shoulder under the beam — easing only at "mein Leiden" into chains of resolving sighs. Then the great recitative: the cello rolling in continuous waves ("my pilgrimage in the world is like a sea-voyage") — and at the words "I step out of the ship into my own city," **the waves simply stop**: flat calm, arrival, one of Bach's perfect theatrical silences. The final chorale ("Come, O death, my slumber's brother") Bach harmonizes with such tenderness that death arrives as family.

**Craft.** A masterclass in **continuo as scenery** — the accompaniment as sea, road, and burden while the voice walks through it; the R4 text-painting essay's central sacred example.

**Why it matters.** Pair with BWV 82 as the two faces of the solo cantata: 82 the mystic's rest, 56 the pilgrim's labor. Fischer-Dieskau to Goerne, every great German bass has measured himself against this pair — a living performance lineage the Arc III cards will pick up.
