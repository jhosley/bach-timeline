**What it is.** A deliberate diptych: the two reigning national styles — **Italy's concerto, France's overture-suite** — each rendered complete on solo two-manual harpsichord, bound and sold as one volume. The keys tell the intent: F major and B minor sit a tritone apart, the two poles of the tonal globe — Europe's musical map, folded into one binding.

**Listen for.** In the *Italian Concerto*: the two manuals as orchestra-versus-soloist — forte manual for the tutti, piano for the solo ([the ritornello engine](../../events/weimar/vivaldi-transcriptions.md) with dynamics built into the instrument's hardware); the slow movement one long Italianate cantilena over a strumming bass, the finale pure Vivaldian engine. Even Scheibe — *[that Scheibe](../../events/leipzig-2/scheibe-1737.md)* — praised it in print as the model of the German concerto. In the *French Overture*: the full Versailles suite ([Celle's lessons](../../events/lueneburg/hamburg-celle-trips.md), forty years matured) — dotted royal entrance, the dance chain at maximum courtliness, ending in a flickering "Echo" movement playing the manuals as near and far.

**Craft.** The R4 national-styles essay in one opus: the same composer, the same instrument, two complete musical civilizations — synthesis by demonstration.

**Why it matters.** Bach's clearest self-conscious display of the alloy his whole style *is* — the education tracks of this timeline (Celle, the Vivaldi crate) presented, at fifty, as finished public mastery.
