**The pair.** Book II's tragic summit, answering [Book I's five-voice bb-minor](../cothen/bwv-867.md) on the same ground: a grieving duet-prelude, then a **four-voice fugue** of terrifying rigor — its angular subject soon confronted with its own **inversion**, the two forms battling through strettos to a climax many consider the most intense page in either book. **Glenn Gould named it among his favorites**; his recording is its modern monument.

**Study.** The upright/inverted drama is fully audible once you know the subject — the rare *learned* fugue whose devices read as raw emotion at first hearing. If Book II has an hour-of-reckoning, this is it; schedule it accordingly.
