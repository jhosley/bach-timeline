**What it is.** The compact jubilation: Psalm 117 — the shortest psalm — for a single four-part choir with independent continuo, all praise, no funeral: fugal exuberance on "Lobet den Herrn," a warmer central paragraph on God's "Gnade und Wahrheit" (grace and truth), and a dancing triple-time **"Alleluia"** to finish.

**Listen for.** The scale of the melismas — single syllables spun into long garlands — and the Alleluia's lift, a minute of pure choral happiness that has ended a thousand concerts.

**The asterisk.** Its sources are late copies and its style slightly anomalous, so authenticity has been questioned — retained by most scholars, flagged here per the timeline's policy. If a doubter someday wins the argument, the loss will be felt at every choir's encore.

**Why it matters.** Proof the motet shelf isn't all mourning — and, with its independent continuo line, another data point in the how-were-these-performed question [BWV 226's parts](bwv-226.md) answer.
