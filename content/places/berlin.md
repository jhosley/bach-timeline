**In Bach's lifetime.** Two Prussian errands bracket his career: [the 1719 trip](../events/cothen/berlin-harpsichord-1719.md) to collect the Mietke harpsichord (and meet [a Margrave](../people/christian-ludwig-brandenburg.md)), and [the May 1747 Potsdam evening](../events/leipzig-3/potsdam-1747.md) with [Frederick the Great](../people/frederick-the-great.md) — "old Bach is come" — with visits to son [Emanuel's](../people/carl-philipp-emanuel-bach.md) household and the new opera house besides.

**After Bach, the custody capital.** Berlin is where the eclipse's relay ran: Bach's old students (Kirnberger, Agricola) teaching his method at the Prussian court; Princess Anna Amalia's library collecting manuscripts; [van Swieten](../events/eclipse/van-swieten-salon.md) exporting the treasure to Vienna; the **Singakademie** hoarding scores — including the Matthew Passion that [a grandmother had copied for a boy](../events/revival/singakademie-score-1824.md). Then, [11 March 1829](../events/revival/mendelssohn-revival-1829.md): the revival's stage. Today the **Berlin State Library** holds the greatest Bach manuscript collection on earth — the [Brandenburg dedication score](../events/cothen/brandenburg-dedication-1721.md), the [Mass](../works/leipzig/b-minor-mass.md), the [Matthew Passion's red-ink autograph](../works/leipzig/st-matthew-passion.md) — the scattered estate's largest reassembly.

**What survives today.** Potsdam's Sanssouci palace and its music room (the fortepiano evening's setting, visitable); the Singakademie building (now a theater); the State Library's treasures, digitized on [Bach Digital](../BIBLIOGRAPHY.md). Berlin never employed Bach; it ended up keeping him.
