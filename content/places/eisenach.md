**The town then.** A ducal residence town of about 6,000 under the Wartburg castle, at the western edge of the Thuringian forest. Musically organized like every German town of its size: a Stadtpfeifer company (the Bachs') holding the legal monopoly on public music-making, a Latin school supplying choirboys, and the Georgenkirche at the center of civic worship.

**Luther's shadow.** Luther went to school in Eisenach and later translated the New Testament at the Wartburg above the town. The Georgenkirche — where Luther had preached, where cousin Johann Christoph Bach played the organ, and where Sebastian was baptized on 23 March 1685 — physically joins the Reformation and the Bach story at one font.

**What Bach's childhood soundscape held.** Tower brass from his father's band; congregational chorales; the school choir (Currende) singing for alms through the streets in winter; and at home, one of the most sophisticated musical households in Thuringia.

**What survives today.** The Georgenkirche and its font; the Wartburg (a World Heritage site); the Bachhaus museum — one of the oldest composer museums in the world (opened 1907), with a period-instrument collection played live for visitors. The actual birth house is not certainly identified; the Bachhaus is the memorial site.
