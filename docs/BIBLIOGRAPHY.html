Bibliography & Sources

The master source list for the Bach Timeline. Every short citation in a content file's `sources` field (e.g. `Wolff, The Learned Musician, ch. 7`) resolves to a full entry here. The app will render this as a Sources & Further Reading section, and each card's footnotes will link into it.

Links policy: book links point to a purchase page (Amazon) verified to resolve at the date noted; public-domain sources point to free full texts; online resources point to their canonical URLs. All links re-verified during phase R6.

Primary sources (documents from Bach's lifetime and circle)

The New Bach Reader

Hans T. David and Arthur Mendel, eds.; revised and enlarged by Christoph Wolff. The New Bach Reader: A Life of Johann Sebastian Bach in Letters and Documents. New York: W. W. Norton, 1998. What it is: the surviving documents in English — council minutes, contracts, Bach's letters (including the 1730 Erdmann letter), the Obituary of 1754, early reception documents. When a card cites a council record or letter, this is where to read it whole. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393319563 (verified 2026-07-06)

Forkel's biography (1802)

Johann Nikolaus Forkel. Johann Sebastian Bach: His Life, Art, and Work (orig. Ueber Johann Sebastian Bachs Leben, Kunst und Kunstwerke, 1802). English trans. Charles Sanford Terry, 1920. What it is: the first Bach biography, built on correspondence with Bach's sons — invaluable and openly partisan (Forkel's nationalist motives are themselves part of the Arc II story). Public domain. Link: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/35041 (verified 2026-07-06)

The modern scholarly spine

Wolff — the standard biography

Christoph Wolff. Johann Sebastian Bach: The Learned Musician. New York: W. W. Norton, 2000; updated ed. 2013. What it is: the standard scholarly life — meticulous on documents, employment, and institutions. This timeline's default authority for biographical fact; chapter citations throughout refer to this book. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393322564 (verified 2026-07-06)

Gardiner — the modern portrait

John Eliot Gardiner. Bach: Music in the Castle of Heaven: A Portrait of Johann Sebastian Bach. New York: Alfred A. Knopf, 2013. What it is: the conductor's Bach — vivid, opinionated, humanizing (the unruly schoolboy, the fractious employee, the visceral theologian), strongest on the vocal works from a lifetime of performing them. This timeline's voice for interpretation is calibrated against it; cited wherever a reading (rather than a fact) is in play. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0375415297 (verified 2026-07-06)

Williams — the skeptical counterweight

Peter Williams. J. S. Bach: A Life in Music. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2007. What it is: a deliberately source-skeptical biography that questions comfortable traditions — the conscience behind this timeline's Documented / Tradition / Disputed flags. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0521870747 (verified 2026-07-06)

Dürr — the cantata bible

Alfred Dürr. The Cantatas of J. S. Bach. Trans. Richard D. P. Jones. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2005. What it is: the standard work-by-work reference for the cantatas — dating, occasion, text, analysis. The R3 cantata cards' primary authority. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0199297762 (verified 2026-07-06)

Focused studies cited by specific cards

Boyd — the Brandenburgs

Malcolm Boyd. Bach: The Brandenburg Concertos. Cambridge Music Handbooks. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1993. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0521387132 (verified 2026-07-06)

Ledbetter — the Well-Tempered Clavier

David Ledbetter. Bach's Well-Tempered Clavier: The 48 Preludes and Fugues. New Haven: Yale University Press, 2002. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0300178956 (verified 2026-07-06)

Dreyfus — the patterns of invention

Laurence Dreyfus. Bach and the Patterns of Invention. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1996. What it is: the modern study of how Bach's composing actually worked — inventio as craft; foundational for the Craft essays. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/0674013565 (verified 2026-07-06)

Applegate — the 1829 revival

Celia Applegate. Bach in Berlin: Nation and Culture in Mendelssohn's Revival of the St. Matthew Passion. Ithaca: Cornell University Press, 2005. What it is: the standard study of the Mendelssohn revival — Arc II's key modern source. Link: https://www.amazon.com/dp/080144389X (verified 2026-07-06)

Snyder — Buxtehude

Kerala J. Snyder. Dieterich Buxtehude: Organist in Lübeck. Rev. ed. Rochester: University of Rochester Press, 2007. Link: https://www.amazon.com/Dieterich-Buxtehude-Organist-Eastman-Studies/dp/1580462537 (verified 2026-07-06)

Online resources

Bach Digital

The Bach-Archiv Leipzig's manuscript and source database — digitized autographs, dating, provenance. The timeline's authority for manuscript facts. Link: https://www.bach-digital.de (verified 2026-07-06)

bach-cantatas.com

The encyclopedic community resource for the vocal works — texts, translations, occasions, discographies. Link: https://www.bach-cantatas.com (verified 2026-07-06)

IMSLP (Petrucci Music Library)

Free public-domain scores; every work card's score link points here. Link: https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Bach,_Johann_Sebastian (verified 2026-07-06)

All of Bach (Netherlands Bach Society)

Free, high-quality filmed performances of Bach's works, growing toward completeness — the timeline's first recommendation for watching this music played. Link: https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/allofbach (verified 2026-07-06)

Monteverdi Choir & Orchestras (Gardiner)

Home of the Bach Cantata Pilgrimage recordings (Soli Deo Gloria label) featured throughout the listening links. Link: https://monteverdi.co.uk/about-us/SDG (verified 2026-07-06)

NASA — Voyager Golden Record

The contents listing for the Golden Record, carrying three Bach works beyond the solar system (Arc III). Link: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/sounds/ (verified 2026-07-06)
