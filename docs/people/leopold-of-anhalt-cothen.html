Prince Leopold of Anhalt-Cöthen

1694 – 1728 · Employer, 1717–1723 (Bach retained the honorary title until Leopold's death)

The only employer Bach ever described with open affection. Writing to his old school friend Georg Erdmann in 1730, Bach recalled: "There I had a gracious Prince, who both loved and knew music, and in his service I intended to spend the rest of my life."

Leopold ruled a principality small enough that his passion could set its priorities. Trained in music from childhood and polished by a grand tour through Italy, he played violin, viola da gamba, and harpsichord and sang bass — well enough to participate in his own Capelle rather than merely fund it. When Prussia disbanded its court orchestra in 1713, the teenage prince snapped up its best players; hiring Bach as Kapellmeister in 1717 completed the project. He paid Bach handsomely, stood godfather to a Bach son (named Leopold Augustus in his honor), and took his Kapellmeister along on his spa trips to Carlsbad — treatment closer to companion than servant.

The relationship cooled after Leopold's 1721 marriage (see the amusa event) and the budget pressures of a small state, but never soured: Leopold released Bach graciously to Leipzig in 1723 and let him keep the Kapellmeister title. Leopold died in 1728, aged just thirty-three. Bach returned to Cöthen in March 1729 to perform funeral music for him — much of it drawn from the St. Matthew Passion, the deepest music he had.
