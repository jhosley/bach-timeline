Glenn Gould

1932 – 1982 · The modernists' Bach incarnate — the 1955 and 1981 Goldbergs

Toronto-born, self-exiled from the concert stage at thirty-one, and the twentieth century's most influential Bach pianist — possibly its most influential Bach listener: Gould heard the counterpoint as living argument and made records on which, for the first time for many listeners, every voice in a fugue is separately, thrillingly audible. The 1955 Goldberg debut made him famous; the studio-hermit decades (humming, splicing, broadcasting, writing) made him a prophet of exactly the recorded, on-demand musical world the completeness era delivered; the 1981 Goldberg remake — released weeks before his death at fifty — closed the frame with late-style inwardness.

His Bach was unapologetically his: piano not harpsichord, tempos from another physics, repeats dropped, romance banished — the historically informed movement's exact contemporary and exact opposite, and the standing proof that the two roads both reach the summit. His WTC II C-major rides the Voyager record; his beloved II/22 anchors that card. For a study companion, he is the essential comparison term: hear any work here in Gould and in a period recording, and the gap between them is a free education in everything interpretation decides.
