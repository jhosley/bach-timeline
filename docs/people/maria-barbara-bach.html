Maria Barbara Bach

1684 – 1720 · First wife (married 1707), mother of 7 of Bach's children

Bach's first wife was also his second cousin — a Bach herself, daughter of the composer Johann Michael Bach of Gehren. They married in October 1707, shortly after Bach took the Mühlhausen post, and she bore seven children in twelve years; four survived infancy, including two of the great composer-sons, Wilhelm Friedemann and Carl Philipp Emanuel.

Almost nothing of her voice survives in the documents — no letters, no portrait — which is itself a lesson in how thin the record of Bach's private life is. What we have is the shape of her presence: the household she ran through the Weimar and early Cöthen years, the family memory (via the Obituary written partly by her son Emanuel) of a happy marriage, and the shock of its ending. In July 1720, while Bach was in Carlsbad with Prince Leopold, she died suddenly at about thirty-five; by the time he returned she was already buried (see the event).

Her death sits at the exact midpoint of the Cöthen years, and students have long been tempted to hear it in the music — most famously in the great Chaconne for solo violin, whose autograph fair copy dates from 1720. That reading is speculation, not documentation (the timeline flags it as tradition), but the biographical fact stands: the most inward music of Bach's instrumental period was written in the shadow of this loss.
