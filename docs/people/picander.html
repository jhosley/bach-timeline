Picander (Christian Friedrich Henrici)

1700 – 1764 · Principal Leipzig librettist — St. Matthew Passion, Coffee Cantata, lost cycle

A Leipzig postal official and occasional-verse entrepreneur who wrote, under the pen name Picander, everything the market wanted — wedding poems, satires (some scandalous), civic flattery — and, for Bach, the words of the St. Matthew Passion. The pairing scandalizes romantic sensibilities: the era's deepest sacred meditation, co-built with a commercial jingle-writer. The documents don't blink; professional text-craft was a trade, Picander was good at it, and Bach clearly found him workable — biddable to structural demands (the Passion's dialogue-and-reflection architecture is compositional thinking imposed on the verse), quick, and local.

The collaboration ran two decades: the Matthew Passion; the (mostly lost) St. Mark Passion; an entire annual cantata cycle (1728–29) of which little survives with music; the secular cantatas' comedy — the Coffee Cantata's caffeinated daughter is Picander at his best — and the Peasant Cantata's dialect fun.

His person-card lesson: Bach's texts came from the working world, not from poets of genius — and Bach's settings routinely transfigure verse that reads, on the page, as competent filler. Where the words are conventional and the music bottomless, you are watching the composer out-read his librettist: one of the most instructive recurring sights in the cantata catalog.
