Georg Erdmann

1682 – 1736 · School friend (Ohrdruf, Lüneburg); lifelong correspondent

The friend of Bach's youth — companion on the 1700 walk from Ohrdruf to Lüneburg and fellow Mettenchor scholar — who left music for law and diplomacy, ending as Imperial Russian resident (consul) in Danzig.

His importance to posterity is outsized: because Erdmann was far away and trusted, Bach wrote him letters — and Bach's surviving personal letters are vanishingly rare. The great one, of 28 October 1730, is the single most quoted document in Bach biography: in it Bach reviews his whole career ("From my youth up... I was destined for music"), explains the move from Cöthen (the amusa passage), confesses his status anxieties about the cantorate, complains precisely about Leipzig's costs, his employers ("strange authorities with little devotion to music"), and — half-seriously — asks Erdmann to find him a job in Danzig. The composer of the St. Matthew Passion, at the height of his powers, was quietly asking a school friend to help him leave.

A person card for a man with no music in the story — because without this friendship, the timeline would lose its best first-person source for how Bach understood his own life.
