Christian Ludwig, Margrave of Brandenburg

1677 – 1734 · Dedicatee of the Brandenburg Concertos

Youngest son of the Great Elector and uncle of the Prussian king, Christian Ludwig kept a modest musical establishment in Berlin and collected music with genuine, if conventional, enthusiasm. He heard Bach play during the 1719 harpsichord-collection trip and asked him to send some compositions; two years later he received the beautifully copied score of the six concertos that now carry his territory's name.

There is no evidence he ever had them performed — his small Capelle likely couldn't cover the works' unusual instrumentations — and no evidence Bach received any reward. At the Margrave's death in 1734 the score was valued at a few groschen and passed through private hands and archives, surviving (narrowly — including a Second World War evacuation) to become one of the most treasured manuscripts in the Berlin State Library. History's little joke: an inattentive dedicatee accidentally preserved, in one immaculate fair copy, an anthology Bach himself never published.
