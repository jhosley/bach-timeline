Frederick II ('the Great'), King of Prussia

1712 – 1786 · Host and challenger of the 1747 Potsdam visit; dedicatee of the Musical Offering

Europe's model modern monarch — philosopher-correspondent of Voltaire, military gambler, flute virtuoso — who kept one of the continent's finest musical establishments at Potsdam with C.P.E. Bach at the keyboard and nightly chamber concerts led from the royal flute. His taste was firmly galant: melody, elegance, feeling — the very fashion that filed old Bach's counterpoint under the past (Scheibe's aesthetics, crowned).

His one evening with the elder Bach (May 1747) became the most famous scene of the composer's life: the royal theme, the improvised fugues on the new Silbermann fortepianos, and the Musical Offering that followed — a monument of the art Frederick's own era was discarding, dedicated to him, and apparently never acknowledged. Decades later, to a visitor, the old king could still sing the theme from memory and tell the story of "old Bach" — the anecdote reaching us through the diplomat's diary: patron and monument had quietly exchanged places.

For the timeline he is the perfect closing foil of Arc I: absolute power, modern taste, and historical glamour — outlived, in the only ledger this app keeps, by a cantor's answer to a party trick.
