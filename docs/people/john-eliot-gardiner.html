Sir John Eliot Gardiner

1943 – · Conductor-scholar; the timeline's principal modern voice

Founder of the Monteverdi Choir (1964) and the English Baroque Soloists (1978), and for half a century one of the defining Bach interpreters alive: rhythmically fierce, dance-rooted, dramatically vivid — the HIP revolution's second generation at full confidence. His grandparents' farmhouse, by family legend he loves to tell, sheltered the great Haussmann portrait of Bach (the canon-holding one) during the Second World War — the future conductor grew up, literally, under Bach's gaze. (Tradition flag on the aura; the painting's wartime residence is documented.)

The 2000 Cantata Pilgrimage is his monument — all the sacred cantatas, on their own Sundays, in one year, self-released on his SDG label when the industry balked — and Music in the Castle of Heaven (2013) its written companion: the performing musician's biography, impatient with plaster saints, alert to the unruly documented Bach, and eloquent on what it feels like to conduct this music at its own pace. This timeline cites him wherever interpretation (rather than document) is at stake, and its default cantata recommendations are his — a deliberate design choice: the study companion's catalog and its living-tradition story should reinforce each other, and no one embodies that union more completely.
