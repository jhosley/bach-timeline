Orphaned: the deaths of Elisabeth and Ambrosius

1694-05-01 – 1695-02-24 · eisenach
documented

Elisabeth Bach was buried on 3 May 1694, cause unrecorded, after twenty-six years of marriage and eight children. Johann Ambrosius, with a household of dependents and a civic post demanding a functioning family workshop, remarried that November — a widow, herself twice bereaved. Three months later, in late February 1695, Ambrosius died at fifty. His widow petitioned the town to keep the music post in the family and was refused; within months the household broke up.

Sebastian was nine at his mother's death, not quite ten at his father's. Of the surviving children, the two youngest boys — Sebastian and Johann Jacob — were sent thirty miles south to Ohrdruf, to their eldest brother Johann Christoph, a twenty-three-year-old organist newly married and modestly paid, who took them in.

The documents give only this administrative skeleton — registers, petitions, a school enrollment. No letter, no reminiscence; the Obituary passes over the whole catastrophe in a phrase. Whatever the boy felt is outside the record, and this timeline won't invent it. But the structural fact belongs on the family track in bold: the composer whose sacred music returns again and again to death as homecoming — Actus tragicus, "Komm, süßes Kreuz," "Ich habe genug" — buried both parents before his tenth birthday, then spent his life inside a liturgy and a theology built to answer exactly that experience.
