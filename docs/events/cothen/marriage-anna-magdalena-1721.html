Cöthen: the instrumental golden age

Marriage to Anna Magdalena Wilcke

1721-12-03 · cothen
documented

On 3 December 1721, in his own quarters and by princely permission (sparing the widower a full public church wedding), Bach married Anna Magdalena Wilcke: twenty years old, daughter of a court trumpeter, and herself a professional singer at the Cöthen court — a soprano well enough regarded to rank among the court's best-paid musicians. The parish record notes the cellar tax on the wine for the celebration; the account books, ever helpful to biographers, show the household's Rhine wine purchase.

For the four motherless children the marriage restored a household; for Bach it began the deepest professional partnership of his life. Anna Magdalena became his principal copyist — her hand eventually nearly indistinguishable from his — his partner in the domestic music-making documented by her two notebooks, and mother of thirteen more children. When the family later moved to Leipzig, the couple functioned as a workshop: Bach composing at speed for the weekly cantata deadline, Anna Magdalena and the older children copying performance parts.

Why it matters. Modern scholarship (and Gardiner's portrait) insists on seeing the Bach household as a musical economy — a family firm in the old Bach-clan tradition — rather than a great man plus dependents. Several major works survive only because of Anna Magdalena's copying, including the cello suites. Her card (person page) carries the story to its bitter end in Arc II.
