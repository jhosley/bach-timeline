Cöthen: the instrumental golden age

A son named for the prince — and lost within a year

1718-11-15 · cothen
documented

In November 1718 Maria Barbara gave birth to the couple's seventh child, a son christened Leopold Augustus — named for the prince, who stood as godfather along with other members of the ruling family. A reigning prince sponsoring his Kapellmeister's child at the font was a marked honor, and a measure of how warm the relationship between Bach and Leopold actually was.

The boy lived ten months, dying in September 1719. He was the fourth of Maria Barbara's seven children to die in infancy or early childhood.

Why it matters. The timeline's family track will show this pattern relentlessly, because it is the constant background of Bach's whole adult life: of his twenty children across two marriages, ten died before adulthood — a rate that was unremarkable for the era. Students sometimes reach for this fact to explain the depth of the funeral cantatas and Passions; the timeline flags such readings as interpretation, not documentation. What is documented is simpler and heavier: there was scarcely a period of Bach's adult life without a recent grave in it, and the Lutheran art of consolation — Actus tragicus, "Komm, süßes Kreuz," "Ich habe genug" — was not an abstract genre for its composer or its first audiences.
