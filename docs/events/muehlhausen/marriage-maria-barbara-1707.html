Marriage to Maria Barbara at Dornheim

1707-10-17 · muehlhausen
documented

In August 1707 a maternal uncle's estate delivered Bach a legacy of 50 gulden — over half a year's salary, and the difference between courtship and marriage. On 17 October 1707, in the small village church of Dornheim outside Arnstadt, Johann Sebastian Bach married Maria Barbara Bach — his second cousin, daughter of the late Johann Michael Bach of Gehren (the "profound" Eisenach cousin's brother, himself a fine composer whose works Sebastian performed and preserved). The officiant was a family friend; the fee, the register notes, was waived.

Marrying a Bach meant the clan reinforcing itself — she knew the trade, its households and hardships, from birth — and her father's manuscripts merging into her husband's library was itself a small dowry of repertoire. If she was indeed the "unfamiliar maiden" of the Arnstadt choir loft, the marriage also closes the era's one romance with documentary charm: the courtship survives, if at all, as a citation for unauthorized music-making.

The first Bach household formed around the Divi Blasii post: within the year came the move to Weimar, then seven children in twelve years — Catharina Dorothea (1708), Wilhelm Friedemann (1710), twins who died at birth (1713), Carl Philipp Emanuel (1714), Johann Christoph Friedrich... the family track carries them from here. Its terminus, Cöthen, July 1720, is already written.

Why it matters. The founding of the family economy that produced, besides the children, the working conditions of everything from the Weimar organ works to the Cöthen teaching books: Bach's composing life and household life were never separate operations.
