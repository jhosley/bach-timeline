Leipzig I: the cantata machine

Liebster Gott, wenn werd ich sterben?

BWV 8 · E major · 1724
documented
Forces — Flute, oboes d'amore, horn (chorale), strings, choir
Occasion — 16th Sunday after Trinity
Dating — 24 September 1724, chorale cantata cycle.
Recommended recording — Gardiner, Bach Cantata Pilgrimage (SDG)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. "Dearest God, when shall I die?" — and Bach answers the dread question with one of his most consoling sound-pictures: the opening fantasia sets funeral bells ringing through a pastoral landscape — pizzicato strings tolling low, the flute chiming high repeated notes above, oboes d'amore warming the middle — death contemplated from a sunlit meadow, E major against every expectation.

Listen for. The bell-layers (count the different tolling speeds); the tenor aria's anxious pulse ("what is my portion?"); and the bass aria's answer — a gigue, dancing the fear away ("what does it matter to me?"), before the plain chorale settles it.

Why it matters. Gardiner singles this cantata out as one of the Pilgrimage's revelations, and it is the chorale cycle's best door into Bach's death-theology for a modern listener: the household context makes it biography; the E-major bells make it art.
