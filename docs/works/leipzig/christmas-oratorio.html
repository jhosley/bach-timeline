What it is. Six cantatas designed as one work — the Nativity narrative from the manger to the Magi, told by an Evangelist across six feast days and bound into a single tonal arch (D major trumpets at both ends). Part II opens with the Sinfonia: the pastoral orchestra of shepherds answered by the angel-choir of oboes — the scene set with no voice at all.

Listen for. The parody pairs (the event card tells the story): Jauchzet, frohlocket began as a drum-fanfare birthday ode, and knowing it makes the transformation more impressive, not less — same notes, higher object. Against the brilliance, the intimacies: the alto's Schlafe, mein Liebster lullaby, and the chorale Wie soll ich dich empfangen — sung to the same melody as the Matthew Passion's O Haupt voll Blut und Wunden: the Passion chorale planted in the cradle scenes, Good Friday audible at Christmas for every Leipzig ear that knew the tune (and they all did).

Craft. The catalog's master-class in parody technique and in chorale as connective tissue across a multi-evening span.

Reception thread. Forgotten with the rest of the vocal music; first revived in Berlin in 1857, a generation after the Matthew — and now, in German-speaking lands, the single most performed Bach work of all: eclipse and return, run on a different clock.
