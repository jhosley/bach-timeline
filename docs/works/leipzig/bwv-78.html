What it is. For many conductors the perfect chorale cantata — and the cycle's best single demonstration of range, because its first two movements could hardly be less alike and are both immortal.

Listen for. Movement one: a chorale fantasia built on a lament chaconne — the same descending chromatic ground as BWV 12/the Crucifixus, repeated twenty-seven times while the hymn tune rides above: Bach fusing his two deepest structural obsessions (chorale, ground bass) into one movement of tragic grandeur. Movement two: the duet Wir eilen mit schwachen, doch emsigen Schritten — "we hasten with weak but eager steps" — soprano and alto literally scampering over a cello walking-bass that trots like happy feet: the most charming ninety seconds in the sacred literature, directly after the most imposing five minutes. Grief and delight, wall to wall, same hymn, same Sunday.

Craft. The juxtaposition is the lesson: affect in Bach is a compositional choice of machinery (ground bass vs. trotting continuo), not a mood he was in. The R4 essays use this pair as their opening exhibit on the doctrine of the affections.

Why it matters. If you play one chorale cantata for a friend to make the case for all of them, the consensus pick is this one.
