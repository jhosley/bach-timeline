Leipzig III: the summing-up

The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book II

BWV 870–893 · All 24 keys · 1742
documented
Forces — Keyboard
Occasion — The 24-key journey, second traversal
Dating — Assembled c. 1739–42 from new work and revised older pieces; no single autograph title page survives — the 'Book II' framing is partly posthumous.
Recommended recording — András Schiff, The Well-Tempered Clavier, Book II (ECM)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. Twenty years after Book I, Bach walked the 24 keys again — and the differences are a portrait of late style. The preludes have grown: many are full two-part sonata-like movements (the galant world acknowledged, absorbed, out-built); the fugues are fewer-voiced on average but denser in thought — more chromatic subjects, more learned devices worn more lightly.

Listen for (first shelf): the F-sharp minor triple fugue — three subjects, the last a chain of sighs, combined at the end with valedictory calm; the E-major fugue's stile antico — Renaissance polyphony visiting the modern book (the Mass's Credo-idiom, keyboard-sized); the B-flat minor pair — Book II's answer to Book I's E-flat minor grief; the D-major prelude's trumpet-and-drum orchestra inside two hands.

Craft. Compare same-key pairs across the books (C minor, or B minor) — twenty years of the same mind on the same terrain: the single best controlled experiment in Bach's development the catalog can offer, and the R4 fugue essay's before/after spine.

Why it matters. With the Goldbergs and Art of Fugue, the late keyboard triptych — and, for Chopin (who practiced from it before every concert) and Shostakovich (whose op. 87 answers it), the book that taught the future what preludes and fugues were for. Individual cards for all 24 pairs arrive with Book I's in the R3 completion batch.
