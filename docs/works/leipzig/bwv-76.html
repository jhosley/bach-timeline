Leipzig I: the cantata machine

Die Himmel erzählen die Ehre Gottes

BWV 76 · C major · 1723
documented
Forces — Trumpet, oboes, viola da gamba (Part II), strings, choir
Occasion — 2nd Sunday after Trinity
Dating — 6 June 1723 — Bach's second Sunday as cantor, paired by design with BWV 75.
Recommended recording — Gardiner, Bach Cantata Pilgrimage (SDG)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)
Listen
  • Search YouTube — Gardiner

What it is. Week two of the Leipzig era, and the twin of BWV 75: same fourteen-movement, two-part plan, same chorale-frame architecture — the pair clearly designed as a double portal to the new regime. Psalm 19 opens it: "The heavens declare the glory of God" — trumpet and choir in bright C-major proclamation, the firmament as fanfare.

Listen for. The opening's two-stage design (festive proclamation, then a striding fugue on "there is no speech nor language"); and Part II's shift of world: after the sermon, an intimate sinfonia with oboe d'amore and viola da gamba — the cosmos scaled down to chamber inwardness, the day's public and private theologies in one work.

Why it matters. Completes the inaugural diptych: hear 75 and 76 back to back (as Leipzig did, two Sundays running) and you hear a composer announcing a program — scale, structure, chorale-thinking — that the next two years delivered at unimaginable pace.
