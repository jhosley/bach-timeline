What it is. The festival suite — trumpets and drums framing gavottes and a bourrée — containing, second, the most beloved single movement in Bach's orchestral music: the Air, for strings alone.

Listen for. The Air's construction: a bass that simply walks — steady octaves, one foot after another — while the first violins sustain one of the longest singing lines ever conceived, the inner voices leaning in suspensions beneath. Serenity as engineering: every part doing something plain, the whole doing something eternal. Then the trumpets return and the suite dances — the sublime bracketed by civic joy, very Leipzig.

Reception thread. Two vectors of fame: Mendelssohn played this suite's music for Goethe while preparing the 1829 revival — the old poet heard "the beginning of the world"; and violinist August Wilhelmj's 1871 arrangement put the Air's melody entirely on the violin's lowest string — "Air on the G string" — the salon transcription that carried this music everywhere (and renamed it, slightly wrongly, forever). The R5 cards pick up both stories.

Why it matters. The gateway drug of Bach's orchestral music for two centuries and counting.
