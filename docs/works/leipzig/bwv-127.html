Leipzig I: the cantata machine

What it is. The chorale cycle approaching Lent — and a fantasia of extraordinary density: while the choir works the day's hymn ("Lord Jesus Christ, true man and God"), the orchestra quotes other chorales beneath it — the Passion chorale prominent among them, and the German Agnus Dei threaded through — Quinquagesima looking straight down the road to Good Friday, three hymns deep.

Listen for. The soprano aria, Die Seele ruht in Jesu Händen — "the soul rests in Jesus' hands" — over pulsing repeated recorder notes (the death-knell made gentle, BWV 8's bells at their tenderest) with a grieving oboe: by wide consent among the most beautiful pages in all the cantatas. Then the shock: the bass's Last-Judgment scene erupts with trumpet — terror and trust, back to back.

Why it matters. Estomihi was Leipzig's audition Sunday — Bach had won the job on this feast two years earlier — and this is the machine's summit treatment of it: layered quotation as theology, a technique the R4 chorale essay anatomizes here.
