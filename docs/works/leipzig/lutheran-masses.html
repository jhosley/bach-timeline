The Four Lutheran Masses (Kyrie–Gloria Masses)

BWV 233–236 · F, A, g, G · 1738
documented
Forces — Choir, orchestra, soloists
Occasion — Leipzig feast-day liturgy (and plausibly Dresden use)
Dating — Late 1730s; nearly all movements parodied from surviving cantatas.
Recommended recording — Philippe Herreweghe, Collegium Vocale Gent, Bach: Missae Breves (Harmonia Mundi)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. Four short Latin Masses — Kyrie and Gloria only, the pair Lutheran Leipzig retained from the Latin rite for feast days (the same format as the 1733 Dresden Missa) — each about twenty-five minutes, six movements, and each assembled almost entirely by parody from his own cantatas.

Listen for. The G-minor (BWV 235) and A-major (BWV 234) are the connoisseurs' pair — the A-major's Qui tollis transforming a cantata aria into something like suspended animation, strings shimmering over a walking bass. Since nearly every source movement survives, these Masses are the catalog's open-book exam on parody: original and re-texting can be compared bar by bar, and the alterations (declamation, affect, key, scoring) are exactly Bach's compositional values, isolated.

Craft. Rehearsals for the summa: the workshop practices — cantata chorus → Mass movement — that build the B minor Mass are all here first, at study-able scale.

Why it matters. Long condescended to ("mere arrangements") and long under-performed, they've been rehabilitated by exactly the insight this timeline keeps pressing: for Bach, re-composition was composition. Anyone studying the B minor Mass should study one of these first — same laboratory, simpler experiment.
