What it is. The documented one: written for the 1729 funeral of the Thomasschule's old rector (predecessor of the combative younger Ernesti — no relation), with Bach's original instrumental parts surviving — strings doubling one choir, winds the other: our best hard evidence for how the motets actually sounded.

Listen for. Romans 8 — "the Spirit helps our weakness... with sighings unutterable" — and Bach takes sighings literally: the double choirs exchange soft, broken figures before gathering into flowing intercession. The two choirs then merge for a four-part fugue ("Der aber die Herzen forschet") and close with a plain, luminous chorale verse — scholar's text, congregation's tune, the whole Lutheran funeral in one arc.

Why it matters. The performance-practice key to the set (voices doubled, not a cappella purism) — and a reminder that these masterworks were occasional labor: a schoolmaster died, the cantor wrote this by the weekend.
