What it is. The gentlest motet, and formally the odd one: no scripture, no chorale — a Leipzig funeral poem, and instead of the usual closing hymn, an "Aria": a simple, swaying strophic song for both choirs as one, "I place myself in Jesus' hands." The genre relaxing its rules for the sake of a farewell.

Listen for. The opening exhalations — "Komm" passed between the choirs like failing breath, the weariness composed into the very spacing of the entries ("the way is long, the strength ever smaller"). Then the turn at "Du bist der rechte Weg" — you are the way — where exhaustion finds a walking pulse. And that closing Aria: after eight-part sophistication, a tune of deliberate, cradling plainness. Sophistication choosing simplicity is late-Bach's deepest gesture; here it comforts mourners.

Why it matters. Death as homecoming — the theology this timeline has tracked since the Actus tragicus — in its most humane choral form.
