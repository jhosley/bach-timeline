The pair. The quiet exit — and the deliberate anti-rhyme with Book I's ending: where 1722 closed the tonal universe with a Largo containing all twelve notes, 1742 closes with a dancing two-part prelude and a light, folk-touched three-voice fugue that simply — after ninety-six pieces across two books — bows out with a Picardy-third smile.

Study. Endings as self-portraits: the young encyclopedist ends in cosmic grief; the old master, having built the Mass and begun the Art of Fugue in this very key-world, ends the "48" with a dance and a major chord. Read the difference however you like — the timeline only insists you notice it.
