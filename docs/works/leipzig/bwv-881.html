The pair. F minor, the grief-key (I/12, the Sinfonia) — revisited in the galant generation's accents: a binary-form prelude of sighing two-note figures and empfindsam (sensitive-style) pathos that could almost be by his son Emanuel, then a three-voice fugue whose energy is nervous rather than tragic.

Study. Late Bach absorbing the new style he supposedly opposed (Scheibe's actual complaint, answered by assimilation): the prelude is the book's clearest bridge to the coming classical era. Compare the three F-minor griefs across twenty years — vocabulary changes, the address doesn't.
