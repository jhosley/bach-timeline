What it is. The hybrid: a French overture-suite that behaves like a flute concerto — the solo flute threaded through every dance, B minor's seriousness (the Mass's key) hosting the set's wittiest music. Presumably written for a virtuoso — the flute was the 1730s' fashionable instrument (Frederick the Great's, notably).

Listen for. The Sarabande's quiet learning: the bass shadows the melody in canon — strictness hidden inside the tenderest dance. The Polonaise and its "Double" (the flute embroidering over the tune moved to the bass). And the exit: the Badinerie — "a trifle," ninety seconds of flying flute mischief that became the instrument's signature encore (and, in the ringtone era, one of the most-heard Bach melodies on earth — Arc III in your pocket).

Why it matters. Late-Bach lightness — contemporary fashion (galant flute, dance charm) fully embraced and fully Bach: the man Scheibe called turgid, ending an orchestral suite with a giggle.
