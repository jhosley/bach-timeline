Leipzig I: the cantata machine

Ich habe genug

BWV 82 · C minor · 1727
documented
Forces — Solo bass, oboe, strings, continuo
Occasion — Feast of the Purification (Candlemas) — Simeon's canticle
Dating — 2 February 1727 (Candlemas); Bach returned to it repeatedly — five versions survive, including soprano and alto transpositions; 'Schlummert ein' was copied into Anna Magdalena's notebook.
Recommended recording — Lorraine Hunt Lieberson, Orchestra of Emmanuel Music, Craig Smith, Bach: Cantatas BWV 82 & 199 (Nonesuch)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. The most beloved of the solo cantatas: old Simeon, having held the infant Christ, asking to die — "I have enough" — one bass voice, one obbligato oboe, strings, three arias. Death as the granting of rest: the theme of Bach's whole funeral art, in chamber scale.

Listen for. The central aria, Schlummert ein, ihr matten Augen — "fall asleep, you weary eyes" — a lullaby for one's own death, its long phrases repeatedly sinking onto silences (the eyes closing, again and again) before the rocking returns. That this slumber-song was copied into Anna Magdalena's notebook — domestic music in a household that buried a child most years — is the most quietly devastating cross-reference in the whole catalog; make of it what you will (the card, as ever, only points). The finale flips grief entire: Ich freue mich auf meinen Tod — "I look forward to my death" — as a dance.

Craft. Bach's revision habit in miniature: five versions across twenty years — bass, soprano-with-flute, alto — the work re-fitted like a favorite coat. Nothing else he wrote did he perform so often.

Why it matters. The single best door into Lutheran death-theology as Bach lived it — and, via Hunt Lieberson's recording (Arc III territory), a modern proof that this music's consolations still land at full force outside any church.
