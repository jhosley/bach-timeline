What it is. The rematch the BWV 61 card promised: same feast, same Luther hymn, ten years later — and a completely different solution. Where Weimar dressed the chorale as a French overture, Leipzig 1724 weaves it into a flowing chorale fantasia: the tune drifting phrase by phrase through an orchestral texture of expectant, circling figures — Advent as longing rather than royal arrival.

Listen for. The old chant-derived tune held in long notes against the modern orchestra — six centuries of church music in one texture; the tenor's suave aria on the mystery of incarnation; the bass's blustering "battle" aria (the hero-Christ striding), horn ablaze.

Why it matters. The timeline's cleanest same-text comparison: put 61 and 62 side by side and you can measure exactly what a decade — Weimar to Leipzig, Konzertmeister to machine-master — changed in Bach's art. Assign yourself the comparison; it's the best single cantata lesson available.
