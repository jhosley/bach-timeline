Mühlhausen: the mission statement

Gott ist mein König

BWV 71 · C major · 1708
documented
Forces — Four separated choirs of voices & instruments: trumpets & drums; recorders & cello; oboes & bassoon; strings; organ
Occasion — Inauguration of the Mühlhausen town council
Dating — Performed 4 February 1708; printed at council expense the same year.
Recommended recording — John Eliot Gardiner, Monteverdi Choir & English Baroque Soloists, Bach Cantata Pilgrimage (SDG)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. Civic splendor by a 22-year-old: psalm verses and hymn for the town council's inauguration, deployed as four spatially separated instrumental-vocal choirs in the Venetian manner — brass here, recorders there, the church itself made into the orchestra. Styled motetto on its title page; the only cantata printed in Bach's lifetime.

Listen for. The spatial games — blocks of sound answering across the room (choose a recording with real separation) — and, at the center, the quiet coup: Du wollest dem Feinde nicht geben, a subdued, chromatic prayer entirely out of scale with the civic pomp around it, the young composer smuggling inwardness into a state occasion. The aria for the retiring 83-year-old burgomaster (Tag und Nacht ist dein) is occasional music with actual tenderness.

Craft. Bach's one surviving essay in the grand polychoral tradition (Gabrieli's lineage via Schütz) — a style he then abandoned; the double-choir Matthew Passion and motets are its distant heirs.

Why it matters. The printed parts made this, for two centuries, the only Bach cantata the world could easily know — Arc II's economics (see the event) in one artifact.
