Weimar: the organ decade and the Italian conversion

Himmelskönig, sei willkommen

BWV 182 · G major · 1714
documented
Forces — Recorder, strings (violas divided), choir, continuo
Occasion — Palm Sunday (coinciding with Annunciation)
Dating — Palm Sunday, 25 March 1714 — the first Konzertmeister cantata.
Recommended recording — John Eliot Gardiner, Monteverdi Choir & English Baroque Soloists, Bach Cantata Pilgrimage (SDG)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. The inaugural work of the Konzertmeister duty: Palm Sunday 1714, the King of Heaven welcomed into Jerusalem — and Bach welcomed into regular cantata production — in one gesture. Chamber-scaled for the Himmelsburg gallery: solo recorder, divided violas, intimacy throughout.

Listen for. The opening sonata: a processional in dotted French-overture rhythm — recorder and pizzicato-like violin painting the donkey-gait entry into the city, royalty at walking pace. Then the new Italian machinery arriving in Bach's church music in real time: ritornello choruses, da capo arias (the alto's Leget euch dem Heiland unter the jewel). The penultimate chorale fantasia weaves Jesu, deine Passion — Palm Sunday already looking toward Good Friday, the liturgical long game Bach never stopped playing.

Why it matters. A dated hinge: church cantata, before and after, split on this Sunday. Study it beside BWV 4 — seven years apart, different species — and the Weimar synthesis (Italy's forms, Germany's chorales, France's rhythms) is audible as a before/after photograph.
