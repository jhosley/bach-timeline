What it is. The work-card twin of the era event: 46 chorale preludes from a planned 164 — each a miniature (one to three minutes), each presenting its hymn once whole, each built on a single accompanying idea chosen as commentary. The organ student's Inventions; the theologian's epigram collection.

Listen for (a first shelf): Durch Adams Fall — the Fall composed as a bass line of falling dissonant sevenths, the serpent coiling in the inner voice (the most cited two bars of text-painting in Bach); Ich ruf zu dir — trio texture of pleading simplicity (its film-fame via Tarkovsky's Solaris belongs to Arc III); In dulci jubilo — Christmas as a canon of carillons; O Mensch, bewein dein Sünde groß — the ornamented-melody masterpiece, grief gilded; Vom Himmel hoch — descending scales everywhere: angels, actually descending.

Craft. The R4 chorale essay's core exhibit (with BWV 4): one technique per piece, affect from figure, the tune never buried — micro-composition as a teachable discipline. Organists still learn the craft from exactly this book, as intended.

Why it matters. The complete Bach aesthetic in the smallest containers he ever built — and the clearest window into how he read the hymns his whole vocal output stands on.
