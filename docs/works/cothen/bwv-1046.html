What it is. The grandest and most courtly of the six — the only one in four movements, ending not with a fast finale but with a minuet-and-trios sequence, like a French court suite grafted onto an Italian concerto. The pair of horns was an outdoor, aristocratic sound (the hunt) barging into chamber music: contemporary ears would have caught the class signal immediately.

Listen for. The friction between the horns' hunting-call idiom and the oboes' and strings' more polite material in the first movement — Bach layers them as simultaneous different musics, a texture more like collage than blend. The violino piccolo (a small violin tuned high) is the set's oddest solo choice, glinting on top. The third movement's main theme returned years later, re-texted, in Leipzig cantata sinfonias — Bach's recycling economy at work.

Craft. A study in ritornello form stretched toward suite logic; also the set's demonstration of maximal orchestration, answered at the far end by No. 6's violin-less darkness.
