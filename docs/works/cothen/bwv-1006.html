What it is. The set's radiant exit: a Preludio of cascading E-major sixteenth notes followed by a garland of French dances — Loure, Gavotte en Rondeau (the famous one), two Minuets, Bourrée, Gigue. After the Chaconne's abyss and the C-major fugue's cathedral, Bach ends the whole Sei Solo in pure light.

Listen for. The Preludio's single unbroken line — no chords to speak of, yet complete harmony, the purest specimen of one voice implying everything. The Gavotte en Rondeau alternates its catchy refrain with contrasting episodes: rondo form made audible for any listener.

Craft. Bach loved the Preludio enough to recycle it twice in Leipzig — most spectacularly as the opening sinfonia of the town-council cantata BWV 29, rescored for organ solo with trumpets, drums, and full orchestra. One violin line expanded to civic splendor with barely a note changed: the timeline's best single example of how sturdy his linear writing is, and a preview of the parody workshop that dominates the Leipzig eras.
