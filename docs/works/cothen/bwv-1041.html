Cöthen: the instrumental golden age

Violin Concerto in A minor

BWV 1041 · A minor · 1720
disputed
Forces — Solo violin, strings, continuo
Dating — DISPUTED. Traditionally Cöthen; the surviving performance materials date from Bach's Leipzig Collegium Musicum years (c. 1730), and some scholars place composition there. Filed under Cöthen per tradition, flagged.
Recommended recording — Isabelle Faust, Akademie für Alte Musik Berlin, Bach Violin Concertos (Harmonia Mundi)  ·  Recordings Shelf
Score — IMSLP (free score)

What it is. The tauter of the two solo violin concertos: Vivaldi's three-movement fast–slow–fast plan and ritornello engine, fully Germanized — every accompanying voice has contrapuntal work to do, and the "tutti vs. solo" boundary keeps dissolving into conversation.

Listen for. The slow movement: an ostinato-like bass figure repeats throughout while the violin builds long arcs above it — the chaconne instinct again, here as aria. The finale is a gigue with a fuse lit under it.

Craft. Set beside an actual Vivaldi concerto, this piece is the cleanest before/after of Bach's Weimar Italian conversion: same form, drastically higher contrapuntal specific gravity. Bach later rebuilt it as a harpsichord concerto (BWV 1058) for Leipzig coffee-house use — the parody workshop again.
