What it is. The transfigured finale, written for a cello with a fifth, higher string (E above the usual A) — on modern four-string instruments it becomes one of the repertoire's great endurance feats, living high in the register the extra string was meant to make easy. Galanterie pair: two Gavottes.

Listen for. Light as the Fifth Suite is dark: D major with open strings ringing everywhere, a Prelude built on a bariolage effect (the same note alternated between stopped and open strings — a shimmer, like light on water). The Sarabande, all multi-voiced chords, is the most hymn-like page in the six suites; many hear the whole suite as the set's arrival — struggle to radiance, an arc students of the Chaconne will recognize inverted.

Craft. The five-string question is a good first encounter with organology (the study of historical instruments) as a live performance issue: what instrument a piece was for changes what it means to play it faithfully. The same question returns at scale in the Arc III historically-informed-performance story.
