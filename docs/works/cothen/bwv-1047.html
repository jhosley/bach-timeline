What it is. A concerto for four soloists who have no business sharing a stage: a high (clarino) trumpet that can bury the others, a recorder that can be buried by anyone, and an oboe and violin in between. The whole piece is an exercise in making radically unequal voices equal — engineering democracy out of acoustics.

Listen for. How Bach solves the balance problem: the trumpet rests entirely in the middle movement (recorder, oboe, violin in an intimate trio over walking continuo), then launches the finale itself with the fugue subject. The clarino part — played without valves, high in the harmonic series where adjacent notes live — remains one of the most feared in the orchestral literature.

Craft. The finale is a proper fugue distributed across the concerto texture — solo voices entering one by one with the subject while the orchestra provides punctuation — a compact demonstration that for Bach, form (concerto) and technique (fugue) are independent layers that can be freely combined. See the fugue craft essay.

Reception thread. The first movement was chosen as the opening music of the Voyager Golden Record (1977) — track one of humanity's self-portrait. Arc III of this timeline picks the story up there.
