What it is. Four normal dance movements (Allemanda, Corrente, Sarabanda, Giga) — and then the Ciaccona: roughly 256 bars, longer than the four together, a continuous chain of variations over a repeating four-bar bass pattern descending through D minor. The most monumental single movement ever written for an unaccompanied instrument.

Listen for. The architecture: a vast D-minor arch, a central turn to D major that arrives like sunrise after thirty-odd variations of gathering weight, and a return to minor that lands with the force of fate. Track the bass pattern — sometimes explicit, sometimes buried, sometimes only remembered — and you are tracking the whole piece.

Craft. The chaconne principle (endless variation over an unmoving foundation) is Bach's deepest formal instinct in one movement — the same mind as the Goldberg ground and the Passacaglia in C minor. The craft essays treat this movement as the meeting point of dance, variation, and implied polyphony.

Reception thread. Brahms, writing to Clara Schumann: "On one stave, for a small instrument, the man writes a whole world of the deepest thoughts and most powerful feelings. If I imagined that I could have created, even conceived the piece, I am quite certain that the excess of excitement and earth-shattering experience would have driven me out of my mind." Busoni's piano transcription became a Romantic monument in its own right (Arc III). The Maria Barbara memorial reading — see her death event — is tradition, not document, and none the less moving for it.
