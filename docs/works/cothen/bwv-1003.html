What it is. The middle sonata: Grave — Fuga — Andante — Allegro. Denser and more severe than the G minor, with the set's most sustained slow movement.

Listen for. The Andante is the celebrated one: a singing melody over a simultaneously bowed repeated bass line — two genuinely independent voices from one bow, the clearest single demonstration in the literature that the violin can be its own accompanist. The Fuga is long and obsessive, its angular subject worked through inversions and episodes with almost keyboard-like thoroughness.

Craft. Bach (or his circle) arranged this sonata for keyboard (BWV 964), and comparing the two is a free lesson: watch what a fuller instrument adds — and how little was actually missing. The Andante belongs in the implied-polyphony essay as the technique's most explicit case: here the polyphony isn't implied at all; it's played.
