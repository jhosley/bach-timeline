What it is. A dance suite with a built-in hall of mirrors: four movements (Allemanda, Corrente, Sarabande, Tempo di Borea), each immediately followed by its own Double — a variation that keeps the harmony and re-clothes it in faster, running figuration. Eight movements from four skeletons.

Listen for. Play a movement and its Double back to back and you are hearing Bach's variation mind in the open: what he keeps (the bass path, the phrase lengths, the cadences) versus what he dissolves (the surface rhythm, the texture). It is the same discipline that later governs the Goldberg Variations — variation over a constant foundation rather than decoration of a tune.

Craft. The Doubles make this partita the set's best teaching piece for harmonic reduction: students can strip each Double back to its parent and watch the voice-leading skeleton emerge. The craft essay on implied polyphony uses the Corrente's Double as its opening example.
