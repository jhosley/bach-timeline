The pair. The pair where the roles blur: the prelude contains a fully developed fugue of its own — a free, harp-like opening flowing into a substantial fugato — making it the most elaborate prelude in Book I; the official three-voice fugue that follows is, by contrast, compact and genial, almost relieved.

Study. A question-piece for the form essay: what makes a prelude a prelude, if it can out-fugue its fugue? (Working answer: function, not content — the freedom to begin.) Compare I/19, the other boundary-blurrer, where the prelude is a strict fughetta.
