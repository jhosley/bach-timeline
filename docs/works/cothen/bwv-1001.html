What it is. The gateway to the set: Adagio — Fuga — Siciliana — Presto, the church-sonata pattern (slow–fast–slow–fast) that all three sonatas share.

Listen for. The opening Adagio is a written-out improvisation — Bach notating, in exact rhythm, the kind of free preluding a violinist would extemporize, chords blooming into runs. The Fuga's subject is terse (a repeated-note knock and a turn), which is the point: short subjects survive the violin's chordal limits. The Siciliana rocks in pastoral 12/8 with the melody and its accompaniment somehow both present in one instrument; the Presto is a moto perpetuo where the "voices" are implied by leaps between registers.

Craft. Bach later arranged the Fuga for lute and for organ — evidence he considered the counterpoint itself, not the violin idiom, the essence. Compare subjects across the three sonata-fugues (G minor: compact; A minor: angular; C major: chorale-derived) as a miniature course in fugue-subject design.
