Mühlhausen, Free Imperial City (Thuringia)

1707–1708 (age 22–23) · Organist of Divi Blasii (St. Blasius)

The town then. A free imperial city — subject to the Emperor alone, governed by its own council of burghers. No duke, no court: civic Lutheranism in its pure form, with two principal churches (Divi Blasii and St. Mary's) and a proud musical tradition recently scarred by a devastating 1707 town fire whose economic damage shadowed Bach's brief tenure.

The church. Divi Blasii (St. Blasius), the councilmen's church, gave Bach a serviceable but aging organ; his surviving rebuild specification — new 32-foot subbass, improved wind, new glockenspiel, technical reasoning stop by stop — is the best single document of Bach the organ engineer, and the council honored it even after he left.

The politics. The clergy's orthodox–Pietist split (see the era essay) made Divi Blasii, whose pastor inclined Pietist, an awkward home for grand church music — the structural reason a triumphant year still ended in resignation.

What survives today. Divi Blasii stands, with a 1959 organ built to Bach's 1708 specification — the nearest thing anywhere to hearing the instrument he designed. The Dornheim village church where he married Maria Barbara (October 1707) survives near Arnstadt and remains a pilgrimage wedding site.
