Berlin (and Potsdam), Prussian residence cities

Visits 1719 (harpsichord), 1741 & 1747 (Emanuel, Frederick); Arc II's custody capital · The Mietke errand, the Potsdam audience — then the manuscripts' refuge and the revival's stage

In Bach's lifetime. Two Prussian errands bracket his career: the 1719 trip to collect the Mietke harpsichord (and meet a Margrave), and the May 1747 Potsdam evening with Frederick the Great — "old Bach is come" — with visits to son Emanuel's household and the new opera house besides.

After Bach, the custody capital. Berlin is where the eclipse's relay ran: Bach's old students (Kirnberger, Agricola) teaching his method at the Prussian court; Princess Anna Amalia's library collecting manuscripts; van Swieten exporting the treasure to Vienna; the Singakademie hoarding scores — including the Matthew Passion that a grandmother had copied for a boy. Then, 11 March 1829: the revival's stage. Today the Berlin State Library holds the greatest Bach manuscript collection on earth — the Brandenburg dedication score, the Mass, the Matthew Passion's red-ink autograph — the scattered estate's largest reassembly.

What survives today. Potsdam's Sanssouci palace and its music room (the fortepiano evening's setting, visitable); the Singakademie building (now a theater); the State Library's treasures, digitized on Bach Digital. Berlin never employed Bach; it ended up keeping him.
