Ohrdruf, County of Hohenlohe-Gleichen (Thuringia)

1695–1700 (ages 10–15) · Ward and pupil of his brother, organist of St. Michael's

The town then. A small Thuringian town of a couple thousand, thirty miles south of Eisenach, notable mainly for its ambitious Latin school (the Lyceum) and its churches. For the Bach clan it was standard territory: the organist post at St. Michael's was held by Bachs for generations.

The household. Johann Christoph Bach's home near St. Michael's: a young organist's family plus two orphaned brothers. Sebastian lived here from age ten to fifteen — the longest he ever lived in any house he didn't head.

The school. The Lyceum's surviving registers track Bach precisely — his class ranks (consistently high, always among the youngest), and his final notation in 1700: departed ob defectum hospitiorum, "for lack of free board." Poverty, recorded in Latin.

What survives today. Little of Bach's Ohrdruf: St. Michael's was destroyed in the Second World War (a ruin/tower remains), and the town is off the main Bach tourist route — fittingly, perhaps, for the most private chapter of the biography.
